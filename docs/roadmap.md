# The next 30 changes (Ryan, 16 October 2026)

Built in this order, one at a time, each tested, committed and published. Ryan's words and answers are in `TASKS.md` under Ryan's decisions.

## A. Booking (1 to 10)

1. **Suggest a card is safe and flat.** It fills the clock and breaks no rules, but ignores chemistry, story timing and promises. It lands around a C. Good shows come from the player.
2. **Battle royals.** (Done 16 October.) Up to 30 people; 30 minutes per 15 (30 people is an hour). Surprise entrants (legends, other companies' talent) can appear. A battle royal night's winner earns a title match at a later big event.
3. **Buildings by city.** (Done 16 October.) Every city has 3 to 5 named buildings (more in big cities). The road picks the one that fits the draw.
4. **Cancellation.** (Done 16 October.) A network can cancel a weekly show after warnings. It becomes a house show (no TV money, a smaller crowd). A strong run of house shows can win a new network deal.
5. **The match pop-up.** (Done 16 October: the Pop-up button beside the pane.) One pop-up books a match: type, people, stipulation, title, length and finish, with the road agent's hint at the bottom. It should look better and be easier than today.
6. **The run sheet.** (Done 16 October for matches: drag, lock in the pop-up, Suggest keeps locked matches.) Drag a match or segment up or down the card. Lock a slot (the opener or the main event) so reordering leaves it alone. Every line keeps a keyboard and remote way to move (rule 5).
7. **What could happen tonight.** A window on Book the show with four parts: possible moments (a title could change, a feud could boil over, somebody could get hurt, the crowd could turn), who is at risk, what the card sets up and leaves cold, and one gut-feeling line. All in words, never a forecast (fog of war).
8. **Sly hints.** The road agent's reads get sharper with a better agent; a weak one says little. Never wrong, never a score.
9. **Book a debut.** When you sign somebody you can book their debut: a vignette, a surprise attack, or a match.
10. **Booking power and action points reset after every show, not every week.** More of them overall, a little carries over, perks raise them, bad actions in the week lose some and good ones earn some back.

## B. Company arcs (11 to 17)

11. **The arc engine.** Picked once a year on Storylines, in the month after the flagship; it builds all year and pays off at the next flagship. Procedurally generated chapters with decisions through the year. It can flop, at a cost, but a flop can be turned around and something saved at the flagship.
12. **The faction takeover.** A group forms and runs roughshod over the company: pick the leader, who joins, who betrays.
13. **The hostile authority.** A new on-screen boss fires people on screen, makes unfair matches, and must be overthrown.
14. **The brand split.** Two shows, a draft, rosters kept apart.
15. **The invasion** as an arc, built on the war that exists.
16. **The plain build to the flagship** as an arc.
17. **Arcs on the Net and in History:** the dirt sheet follows the arc; History keeps every year's arc and how it ended.

## C. The GM (18 to 22)

18. **Research** how Path of Exile, Football Manager, Motorsport Manager and similar games make a manager's growth fun; write `docs/research/gm-progression.md`.
19. **The skill tree.** Five branches by role: Creative, Talent relations, Business, Production, Office politics. Small nodes and a few keystones that change how you play.
20. **Points.** Levels from good shows (grades, sell-outs, ratings, finished stories), milestones (firsts), arc payoffs. Respec at a cost.
21. **Notifications** that celebrate a level, a milestone, a perk.
22. **The legacy score**, in the manner of a long-running sports game's: ratings, hall of famers you signed, and other facts kept for a career.

## D. Decisions that feel big (23 to 25)

23. **Concepts:** three or four looks for a decision (light-board scenes, typed-out text, a sound style of its own), shown to Ryan to pick one.
24. **Build the picked one** on the events after a show and in the week: big, easy buttons.
25. **One big event a show, two or three small ones a week.**

## E. A living show and world (26 to 30)

26. **Research** how simulation and tycoon games make the simulation fun to watch; write `docs/research/living-show.md`.
27. **The show tells one story:** commentary that remembers last week and the week before, and respects what comes next; catchphrases, jokes; fake commercials from your sponsors; backstage cut-ins; the crowd as a character; surprises nobody booked that come from people's reasons.
28. **Contracts by appearance, 6 months, 1, 2 or 3 years**, with realistic money.
29. **Rivals keep feuds, raid your stars and react to your moves**; wrestlers have lives outside the ring.
29b. **Wrestlers remember each other** (Ryan, 16 October: for later). The same memory the booker has (`youRemember`) between wrestlers: who cost whom a title, who carried whom, who betrayed whom, and it bears on what they do next (who they help, who they refuse to work with, who they turn on).
30. **Careers:** no end unless you retire; fired, you can take a job at another company; after ten years you can start your own company with backing from a network or a sponsor. Goals each year from the owner, and the long goal of becoming number one.

Sound (Ryan: "we are going to have to figure out how to make the sound better") sits with 23 and 27.
