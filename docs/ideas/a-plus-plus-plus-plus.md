# The A++++ Plan: 100 ideas to ponder

17 October 2026. A list for thinking, not a plan. Nothing here is in TASKS.md, NEXT.md or any plan, and nothing is promised. Some may overlap ideas already done or listed; that is fine. Ryan picks what, if anything, moves on.

Every idea would still follow the house rules: original content, no dice wording, the engine owns the game, seeded and repeatable, no outside assets.

## 1. Out of the box

- **1. The booker's own body.** You are a person too: stress, sleep, family. Burn out and your decisions get worse; take a holiday and the owner runs a show without you.
- **2. Kayfabe mode.** Play a whole career believing it is real: no ratings, no star numbers, only what the crowd, the press and the people tell you.
- **3. The time machine.** Start a world in any decade from 1950 to today: the tools, the money, the TV and what crowds want change with the era.
- **4. Booking from the bottom.** Start as a ring crew member and work up to booker: set up the ring, then run the merch table, then become a road agent, then get the pencil.
- **5. The haunted arena.** A building with a story of its own: legends who wrestled there, a curse on champions, a crowd that remembers.
- **6. Dream matches from the archive.** Two wrestlers from different decades of your own save meet in a "what if" exhibition, scored from their peak.
- **7. The newsletter you write.** Become the dirt sheet: a mode where you report on a world the game runs, and your scoops move the business.
- **8. The rival booker as a character.** The person booking the other company has a name, a style, a grudge against you, and remembers every war.
- **9. Two bookers, one company.** You and an AI co-booker share the pencil and argue over the card, like a writers' room for two.
- **10. Legend's last run.** Play as a retired great who comes back as booker of a dying company that once made them famous.

## 2. Tycoon systems

- **11. Build your own arena.** Buy land, choose seats, add a video wall, a merch store, a hall of fame wing; it earns on nights you do not run.
- **12. Merchandise lines.** Design shirts, toys and video games for your stars; each line sells by popularity and fades when the star does.
- **13. Your own training school.** A building with coaches, a ring and dorms; better facilities make better graduates and draw better students.
- **14. A TV studio of your own.** Stop renting: own the cameras and the truck, lower production costs, sell studio time to others.
- **15. Pay-per-view to streaming to broadcast.** Choose how to sell your big nights; each era of the business changes which pays.
- **16. Tour buses and travel.** Plan the road like logistics: flights, buses, hotels, the cost of a long loop against a short one.
- **17. Sponsor portfolio.** Many sponsors at once, each wanting different things, some clashing with each other and with your stars.
- **18. Buy a rival.** Acquire a failing company: its tape library, its roster, its debts and its angry locker room.
- **19. Shares and a board.** Go public; a board with members who want growth, profit or prestige, and a vote that can remove you.
- **20. Real estate on the road.** Long leases on buildings in your best markets; cheaper nights, but you are locked in.

## 3. Leveling and progression

- **21. Booker levels.** Experience from every show; each level unlocks a new tool, a bigger budget or a new kind of call.
- **22. Wrestler growth paths.** Choose how each wrestler improves: in the ring, on the microphone, or in character work, and watch them become a different act.
- **23. Reputation by city.** Each city remembers you as a booker, separate from the company: a good name in one town opens doors there.
- **24. A legacy score.** Every career builds a score from titles made, stars built, money earned and moments remembered; compare careers across saves.
- **25. Hall of fame you induct.** Each year choose who goes in; the ceremony is a big night and the inductees' friends remember who you picked.
- **26. Prestige for belts.** A belt levels up with great reigns and great matches, and loses lustre with bad ones.
- **27. Show standing tiers.** A weekly show that climbs from local to institution unlocks bigger guests, a bigger slot and a bigger budget.
- **28. Staff that level up.** Road agents, writers and trainers gain experience with you; a coach you keep for years becomes a legend.
- **29. Mastery badges.** Book ten great openers and earn the "Hot start" badge; badges change how the crowd greets your shows.
- **30. New game plus.** Finish a career and start again with one thing carried over: a protege, a reputation, a grudge.

## 4. Skills and perks for the booker

- **31. A skill tree.** Branches for business, story, people and production; points from experience, one choice at a time.
- **32. "Silver tongue".** A better chance when you ask a star to take a loss or a pay cut.
- **33. "Eye for talent".** See potential in free agents sooner and read the road agent's notes in more detail.
- **34. "Showman".** Promos and angles you book land a little better with the crowd.
- **35. "Penny pincher".** Lower production costs, but the crowd notices cheap shows sooner.
- **36. "Locker room's friend".** Moods recover faster, but the owner trusts you less.
- **37. "Network darling".** Executives are more patient, and bidding wars come more often.
- **38. "Ironman booker".** Run more shows a week without wearing yourself down.
- **39. "Old school".** Long feuds pay more; quick stories pay less.
- **40. Flaws as well as perks.** Pick one weakness at the start (a temper, a gambling habit, a soft spot for veterans) for extra skill points.

## 5. Multiplayer and social

- **41. Head-to-head season.** Two players each run a company in the same world, on the same nights, fighting for the same viewers.
- **42. Shared world league.** Eight friends, eight companies, one week a day; the world advances when everyone has booked.
- **43. Trading talent.** Players trade wrestlers, lend them for a show, or co-own a champion.
- **44. Invasions between players.** One player's company invades another's show; both book their side of the war.
- **45. Co-op booking.** Two players run one company: one books, one runs the business, both answer the owner.
- **46. Hot seat on one screen.** Pass the controller between shows; a night of friends running rival companies.
- **47. Ghost rivals.** Race another player's saved career in the same world: their results appear as a rival company.
- **48. Weekly challenge seed.** Everyone gets the same world and goal each week; a leaderboard of grades and money.
- **49. Spectator mode.** Friends watch your show on the air and vote in the chat room; the crowd's mood leans with them.
- **50. Fantasy draft.** Start a league with a draft of every wrestler in the world, picked in turns.

## 6. Add-ons and new modes

- **51. Play as a wrestler.** A career from training school to main event; you choose moves, promos and whom to trust, and a booker who may not like you.
- **52. Play as the owner.** Hire and fire bookers, set budgets, and watch your choices from the top.
- **53. Indie circuit mode.** Run tiny shows in halls and bars; no TV, all gate, and the chance to be the place stars start.
- **54. Women's company mode.** A company built around a women's division, with its own history and challenges.
- **55. Tag team world.** A world where tag wrestling is the main draw, with team chemistry at the centre of everything.
- **56. Tournament mode.** A single-elimination or round-robin event, booked night by night, with a bracket you can steer.
- **57. Lucha league.** Masks, trios, mask-against-hair matches and a culture where losing a mask is the end of a story.
- **58. Strong style league.** Win-loss records matter, rankings decide title shots, and the crowd watches for respect.
- **59. Hardcore federation.** Weapons, buildings that get wrecked, insurance bills and a crowd that wants more each time.
- **60. Documentary mode.** After a career, the game cuts your history into a "documentary" of chapters, quotes and turning points.

## 7. The show and presentation

- **61. The dot-matrix match viewer.** Matches play out as moving lights: entrances, near falls, a belt raised.
- **62. Entrance builder.** Music style, lights, pyro, a walk or a run; a good entrance lifts the match before the bell.
- **63. The commentary booth.** Two announcers whose lines come from the story; a great team makes a flat match better.
- **64. Crowd signs.** Signs in the crowd made from the story ("We want the rematch"); the camera finds them in the replay.
- **65. Chants.** The crowd chants for people and moments, from the history; a chant can tell you a turn is ready.
- **66. Replays and highlights.** After a show, a reel of its best moments, kept in a library you can watch again.
- **67. The poster maker.** Every big event gets a poster built from the main event, the city and the date, in the game's look.
- **68. A season trailer.** At the end of a year, a short reel of the year's best moments in lights.
- **69. Weather and outdoor shows.** Summer stadium shows with weather on the night, and the risk that comes with it.
- **70. Television graphics.** Lower thirds, tale of the tape and title histories on the broadcast, all drawn by code.

## 8. Stories and characters

- **71. Gimmick changes.** Repackage a wrestler: new name, new look, new music; the crowd may love it or reject it.
- **72. Real-life romances and breakups.** Relationships backstage that the crowd does not see, until they leak or you write them in.
- **73. Generational families.** Children of wrestlers come up through the school with their parents' names and grudges.
- **74. Factions with leaders.** Groups that grow, split and fight for control, with a leader whose mood steers the group.
- **75. Masks and secrets.** A masked wrestler whose identity is a secret the press wants; unmasking is a one-time moment.
- **76. Retirement tours.** A star's last year booked as a farewell, city by city, ending on a night you choose.
- **77. Comebacks.** Retired legends who come back once, for money, for a grudge, or because the crowd begged.
- **78. Managers and valets.** Talkers who make a quiet wrestler a star, and leave with the money when they turn.
- **79. Shoots gone wrong.** A promo that goes off the script and becomes real, with real fallout.
- **80. A story bible.** A page that writes your company's history as a story: eras, heroes, villains, turning points.

## 9. The world and economy

- **81. A living industry.** Companies rise and fold, merge and split, without you; the world map changes over decades.
- **82. Economic cycles.** Booms and busts that change ticket prices, sponsor money and TV fees.
- **83. Wrestling's popularity tide.** The whole business has hot and cold eras; ride a boom or survive a bust.
- **84. International tours.** Take the company abroad, with exchange rates, local stars and crowds that want different things.
- **85. Working agreements.** Lend and borrow talent with a company overseas; their stars visit, yours learn new styles.
- **86. Unions and contracts.** Wrestlers organise; collective demands, a strike threat, and a deal that changes everyone's pay.
- **87. Regulators and athletic commissions.** Rules that change by state or country: medical checks, blood bans, licence fees.
- **88. Scandals beyond wrestling.** Lawsuits, a star's arrest, a sponsor pulled by a news story.
- **89. Rival media.** A rival company's own dirt sheet, spinning stories about you.
- **90. Fan clubs and conventions.** Fan events that make money, build loyalty, and let wrestlers meet the people who cheer them.

## 10. Modding, community and platform

- **91. Steam Workshop worlds.** Share worlds, scenarios, story chapters and logos, with ratings and collections.
- **92. A scenario editor.** Build goals, events and endings without code, and share them as challenges.
- **93. Storyline packs.** Community-written storyline templates the sifting can use.
- **94. A modding API for events.** Simple rules ("when a champion loses at home, then...") that modders write as data.
- **95. Season-end sharing.** A card of your year (grades, best match, money) to share as an image.
- **96. Achievements with stories.** Each achievement tells a small story ("The Night the Lights Went Out: kept the show going in a blackout").
- **97. Daily booker's puzzle.** One card, one crowd, one goal, a new one each day.
- **98. Accessibility options.** Text size, colour-blind palettes, reduced motion, a screen-reader mode for the menus.
- **99. Controller-first TV mode.** A full couch experience: big type, a radial menu, the guide speaking on the screen.
- **100. A companion app.** Outside the game itself (the game stays wide-screen only): check your company on a phone between sessions: the dirt sheet, the chat room, the mail from the owner.
