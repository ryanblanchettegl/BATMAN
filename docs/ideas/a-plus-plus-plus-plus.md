# The A++++ Plan: 200 ideas to ponder

17 October 2026. A list for thinking, not a plan. Nothing here is in TASKS.md, NEXT.md or any plan, and nothing is promised. Ryan picks what, if anything, moves on.

## How this list was checked

Every idea was checked against what is already built or planned, and anything that overlapped was cut:

- WISHLIST.md (all 101), TASKS.md (open and done), NEXT.md, docs/roadmap.md (the next 30 changes, including the skill tree, points and the legacy score), docs/feature-list.md, docs/feature-opportunity-backlog.md, every plan in docs/plans and docs/proposals.
- The add-ons run: eras, kayfabe, journalists, the writers' room, post-mortems, venues and disasters, supercards, the academy, dynasties, the match viewer, procedural audio, file-based leagues, play as a wrestler, scenarios, career saves.
- The nine company models (including the lucha, joshi, outlaw and sport-first companies).
- Today's documents: the first-month guide, story mode, the stat trim, characters with backstories, the playtest, the balance dashboard, Steam saves and achievements.

About 70 of the first list's 100 overlapped and were replaced. If one still brushes against something planned, it is the thinnest of overlaps.

Every idea would still follow the house rules: original content, no dice wording, the engine owns the game, seeded and repeatable, no outside assets.

## 1. Out of the box

- **1. The booker's own body.** You are a person too: stress, sleep, family. Burn out and your choices get worse; take a holiday and the owner runs a show without you.
- **2. Kayfabe mode.** A whole career with no numbers anywhere, only what the crowd, the press and the people tell you.
- **3. From ring crew to booker.** Start by setting up the ring, then run the merch table, then become a road agent, then get the pencil.
- **4. The newsletter you write.** A mode where you are the reporter, not the booker: your scoops move the business the game runs.
- **5. The booker's diary.** Write a line after each show; years later the game quotes your own words back in speeches and retrospectives.
- **6. Superstitions.** Wrestlers keep rituals (lucky boots, a pre-show meal). Break one and moods dip; a streak that follows becomes a legend.
- **7. Time-lapse years.** Fast-forward a year as a montage, stopping only for the three choices that matter most.
- **8. The road not taken.** Once a month after a big night, read a short account of what the other answer would have led to.
- **9. Silent night.** One show a year with no commentary and no music, only crowd noise. The crowd decides everything.
- **10. The prophecy.** An old-timer predicts something each January ("the kid in the gym will be champion"); the game tracks whether it comes true.
- **11. Your past self.** Your company from year one returns as a ghost rival that judges what you have become.
- **12. Hard winter.** A month announced in advance where everything costs more and crowds thin; plan to survive it.
- **13. The lost tape.** A missing match from the history turns up; airing it changes how a legend is remembered.
- **14. Late-night radio.** A call-in show where fans and wrestlers talk, so you hear the room's mood in voices.
- **15. The impostor.** Someone claims to be a famous masked legend's chosen successor; you decide whether to back the claim.
- **16. Swap night.** Once a year wrestlers trade personas for fun; the crowd's reaction reveals hidden potential.
- **17. The sports psychologist.** A staff member who can heal real heat between two people, or help turn it into a story.
- **18. Booking blind.** A challenge where next week's card is locked before you see this week's result.
- **19. The fan in the front row.** One regular followed over years, who one day walks into your tryouts.
- **20. Guest booker.** Run a rival company for one month as a guest, then return to your own and face what you set up.

## 2. Tycoon and business

- **21. Build your own arena.** Buy land, choose seats, add a video wall and a store; it earns on nights you do not run.
- **22. Your own TV studio.** Stop renting: own the cameras and the truck, cut production costs, rent studio time to others.
- **23. Buy a rival.** Acquire a failing company with its tape library, roster, debts and angry locker room.
- **24. Shares and a board.** Go public; board members want growth, profit or prestige, and a vote can remove you.
- **25. Long leases.** Lock in buildings in your best markets for years: cheaper nights, less freedom.
- **26. Concessions.** Run the food and drink stands; price them, and a sold-out night pays twice.
- **27. Season tickets.** Sell a year of shows up front in your best cities, with promises about who will appear.
- **28. VIP packages.** Meet-and-greets that earn well but tire the wrestlers who do them.
- **29. Insurance.** Pay premiums against injuries, disasters and lawsuits; every claim raises the price.
- **30. A record label.** Release entrance themes; a hit theme sells and lifts the wrestler who owns it.
- **31. A video game of your roster.** License your company to a game maker; wrestlers fight over their ratings in it.
- **32. Themed bars.** Open bars in strong markets that carry your brand and your stars' photos.
- **33. Talent agents.** Some wrestlers come with agents who negotiate harder and take a cut.
- **34. Naming rights.** Sell the name of your arena or your flagship to a sponsor.
- **35. A pension fund.** Look after retired wrestlers; a generous fund steadies the whole room.
- **36. The crew as people.** Camera, lighting and sound crews with skills, wages and bad days.
- **37. Price wars.** Undercut a rival's tickets in a shared city and see who blinks first.
- **38. Charity shows.** Lose money on the night, gain goodwill with a city and the press.
- **39. Rights by region.** Sell your shows to different countries one by one.
- **40. Rescue, not game over.** Go broke and a rescue buyer arrives, with conditions you must live with.

## 3. Progression and mastery

- **41. Staff who grow.** Road agents, writers and trainers gain experience with you; a coach kept for years becomes a legend.
- **42. New game plus.** Finish a career and start again with one thing carried over: a protege, a reputation, a grudge.
- **43. Your booking style, named.** The game names your style from what you do (the patient storyteller, the hot-shotter) and crowds learn to expect it.
- **44. The apprentice.** Train a young assistant who later runs your second show, or a rival company.
- **45. Rivals who study you.** After a year, an AI booker adapts to your habits.
- **46. The trophy room.** Objects from great nights (a broken chair, a bell, a torn shirt) kept in your office.
- **47. Mentorship chains for staff.** Your road agent trains the next agent, who carries their habits.
- **48. Learning a city.** Over years your reads of a market's taste get sharper and more specific.
- **49. Earned match types.** Run a few cage matches well before the big cage match truly draws.
- **50. Locker-room lore.** Stories about you spread ("the booker who paid the hospital bill"); newcomers arrive having heard them.
- **51. Self-set handicaps.** Optional rules for a year (no rematches, no title changes) that the owner rewards.
- **52. The longest run.** A ranking of your longest unbroken tenures as booker across all saves.
- **53. Signature spots.** After enough matches a wrestler invents a spot; it becomes theirs and the crowd waits for it.
- **54. Anniversaries.** Your first show, first sell-out and first champion come round each year, and the company marks them.
- **55. Stipulation specialists.** Wrestlers known for ladders or cages, who draw more in those matches.
- **56. The redemption year.** After a bad year, a special set of goals that restores the owner fully if met.
- **57. Scouting trips.** Send a scout abroad for weeks; they come back with a report on talent nobody knows.
- **58. Your best moments as puzzles.** Finish a career and replay its key moments as booking puzzles.
- **59. Press honours.** "Booker of the decade" and other titles from the press, earned over years.
- **60. Fan mail.** The amount of mail tells you who is rising before any number does.

## 4. Multiplayer and social

- **61. Head-to-head season.** Two players each run a company in the same world, on the same nights.
- **62. Trading talent.** Players trade wrestlers, lend them for a show, or share a champion.
- **63. Invasions between players.** One player's company invades another's show; both book their side.
- **64. Co-op booking.** Two players, one company: one books, one runs the business, both answer the owner.
- **65. Hot seat.** Pass the controller between shows; a night of friends running rival companies.
- **66. Ghost rivals.** Race another player's saved career in the same world as a rival company.
- **67. Spectator mode.** Friends watch your show on the air and vote in the chat room; the crowd leans with them.
- **68. Fantasy draft.** Start a world by drafting every wrestler in turns.
- **69. Play by mail.** Take turns whenever you like; the world waits for everyone.
- **70. Talent exchange.** Lend a rising star to a friend's world and get them back changed.
- **71. The world feed.** Other players' biggest moments, if they share them, as news on your Web page.
- **72. Booker guilds.** Players form groups with shared rankings and challenges.
- **73. Same week, side by side.** Two players book the same week of the same world live and compare.
- **74. Stars who travel.** Export a star from your world; they turn up as a free agent in a friend's.
- **75. A rookie draft between players.** Each year a shared class of newcomers, picked in turns.
- **76. A friend on commentary.** A second player picks the commentary lines on your show as it airs.
- **77. Match of the year, voted.** Friends vote across all their saves for the best match.
- **78. Mentor mode.** An experienced player watches a new player's game and leaves notes on their desk.
- **79. Grudge nights.** Two player companies run on the same night; the better-booked show wins a prize.
- **80. One record book.** A group's seasons kept together as one shared history.

## 5. New modes

- **81. Tag team world.** A world where tag wrestling is the main draw and team chemistry is everything.
- **82. Your career as a documentary.** After a career, the game cuts your history into chapters, quotes and turning points.
- **83. One legendary night.** A short mode: a whole year's build compressed into a month for one show.
- **84. Commentator career.** Call matches, build chemistry with partners, get hired by bigger companies.
- **85. The agent.** Guide one wrestler's career, negotiating with every company in the world.
- **86. Sandbox mode.** Change any number mid-game to see what happens; such saves are marked as experiments.
- **87. The contest season.** A season-long competition for a contract, with eliminations you book.
- **88. A puzzle campaign.** Fifty hand-made booking puzzles, each one card and one goal.
- **89. Speed runs.** A fixed world and a goal; reach it in the fewest weeks, with a leaderboard.
- **90. Terminal mode.** The whole game as a green-on-black terminal, for fun.
- **91. The other side of the war.** Replay a war from the rival company's side.
- **92. The pitch meeting.** Pitch a year of stories to a network to win a deal.
- **93. A charity tournament.** Every company in the world enters one tournament for a cause.
- **94. The legends tour.** Book a nostalgia tour of retired greats only.
- **95. A family show.** A second weekly show for children, with its own rules and merchandise.
- **96. Late-night show.** A show with looser rules on a late network, and a crowd that wants more.
- **97. Big nights only.** A company with no weekly TV: only monthly big events.
- **98. The mystery season.** Who attacked the champion? You write the answer while fans guess on the Web.
- **99. Story-only mode.** Matches resolve on their own; the game is the people and the stories.
- **100. Rewatch.** Replay any past show from your save, with fresh commentary.

## 6. The show and presentation

- **101. The poster maker.** Every big event gets a poster from the main event, the city and the date, in the game's look.
- **102. A season trailer.** A short reel of the year's best moments in lights.
- **103. Television graphics.** Lower thirds, tale of the tape and title histories on the broadcast.
- **104. Ring design.** Choose the apron, ropes and turnbuckle pads; your ring is your brand.
- **105. Lighting cues.** Set the lights for big entrances: a dark arena for a monster, a white flash for a hero.
- **106. The camera director.** Pick the shots for a big moment: hard camera, ringside, the crowd.
- **107. The ring announcer.** A character with a famous line and a voice the crowd waits for.
- **108. A pyro budget.** Spend on fire and fireworks show by show.
- **109. The opening video.** Each show opens on a package built from the week's stories.
- **110. The pre-show panel.** A kickoff with analysts predicting results, who are sometimes wrong in public.
- **111. Split screen.** What happens backstage while a match is on.
- **112. The video wall.** Promos and graphics on the in-arena screen.
- **113. A minute-by-minute chart.** After each show, how the audience rose and fell, segment by segment.
- **114. Belt designer.** Design each title's plates, strap and colours.
- **115. Ring gear designer.** Design each wrestler's colours and gear.
- **116. Magazine covers.** A monthly cover picking who looks hottest.
- **117. Ad breaks.** Short sponsor spots between segments, which a sponsor can complain about.
- **118. Holiday shows.** Seasonal sets and themed matches for the holidays.
- **119. The flagship stage.** Choose the scale of the stage for your biggest night.
- **120. Network promos.** The network airs trailers for your shows; you choose what they show.

## 7. Stories, people and drama

- **121. Friends across the war.** Two best friends working for rival companies at war.
- **122. Pranks and initiation.** Locker-room pranks and rookie initiation, with consequences when it goes too far.
- **123. Getting help.** Wellness and addiction handled seriously, with a support programme you can fund.
- **124. Visa trouble.** Foreign stars who cannot travel for a show, and how you work around it.
- **125. Body transformation.** A wrestler changes their look over months; the crowd notices.
- **126. Feuds among staff.** Two writers or agents who cannot stand each other.
- **127. Hidden talents.** A wrestler turns out to sing, or draw, or speak another language; a one-off segment shows it.
- **128. Weddings.** A wedding among the roster, and the choice of whether to put it on the show.
- **129. Hero fatigue.** A hero the crowd has grown tired of, who needs reinventing without a turn.
- **130. Drift.** A wrestler slowly becomes something between hero and villain on their own.
- **131. Ghostwritten promos.** A quiet wrestler gets a writer's voice; the crowd notices if it sounds too polished.
- **132. The long con.** A villain plants a story for a year (a fake injury, a fake retirement) with a planned payoff.
- **133. The locker room's code.** Unwritten rules the room enforces on newcomers.
- **134. Legends on the phone.** Retired greats who call you with opinions about who should be champion.
- **135. Family emergencies.** A wrestler must leave mid-tour; how you handle it is remembered.
- **136. Bodyguards.** Enforcers as a role, protecting a star and starting fights.
- **137. The wrestler who wants your job.** A veteran pitches cards to you and wants the pencil one day.
- **138. A city's grudge.** A town that hates one wrestler for something said there years ago.
- **139. Ring rust.** Wrestlers back from a long break need a few matches to find their timing.
- **140. Character journals.** A wrestler's own account of their year, written from what they remember.

## 8. The world and simulation

- **141. Economic cycles.** Booms and busts that move ticket prices, sponsor money and TV fees.
- **142. Unions and strikes.** Wrestlers organise; demands, a strike threat, and a deal that changes pay for everyone.
- **143. Fan clubs and conventions.** Fan events that earn money, build loyalty, and let wrestlers meet fans.
- **144. Seasons of the year.** Summer crowds, winter travel, a holiday dip in viewers.
- **145. Other big events.** A national final on the same night drains your audience.
- **146. Local politics.** A city bans a match type; lobby, comply, or tour elsewhere.
- **147. New media arrives.** Over a long career new ways to watch appear and old ones fade.
- **148. Generations of talent.** A golden generation, or a dry spell when the pool runs thin.
- **149. Better medicine.** Injury care improves over decades, and careers get longer.
- **150. A rival sport.** A fighting-sport league rises and steals fans and talent.
- **151. Fans who switch.** Fans move between companies, carrying loyalties and grudges.
- **152. Pirated streams.** A drain on streaming income that you can fight, at a cost.
- **153. Busy arenas.** A concert has the building on your preferred date.
- **154. Cost of living.** Wages and prices differ by region.
- **155. Celebrity owners.** A rich fan buys a rival and spends wildly.
- **156. The league table.** Every company ranked each year, published like a sports table.
- **157. Local legends.** A regional hero who draws only in their own city.
- **158. From other sports.** Amateur wrestlers and other athletes enter the free-agent pool.
- **159. Media mergers.** Networks merge and your deal changes overnight.
- **160. Resale prices.** What tickets sell for on the resale market shows true demand.

## 9. Tools, modding and platform

- **161. Event rules for modders.** Simple rules written as data ("when a champion loses at home, then...").
- **162. A season card.** A card of your year (grades, best match, money) to share as an image.
- **163. Accessibility options.** Text size, colour-blind palettes, reduced motion, screen-reader menus.
- **164. A companion app.** Outside the game itself (the game stays wide-screen only): the dirt sheet and the owner's mail on a phone.
- **165. A history file.** Share a whole save's history as a small file friends can browse.
- **166. A booking notebook.** Notes, tags and reminders inside the game.
- **167. Shortcuts and macros.** For players who book fast.
- **168. A "why" on every number.** Press any number to see what moved it.
- **169. Export the books.** Your company's money to a spreadsheet.
- **170. World codes.** A short code that starts someone else's exact world.
- **171. Language packs.** Translation support with a pack format.
- **172. Local formats.** Currency and dates in the player's own style.
- **173. A world checker for modders.** A command-line tool that checks a world file.
- **174. Mod load order.** A manager for several mods and their conflicts.
- **175. Photo mode.** Capture a lights board or a moment and share it.
- **176. Streamer mode.** Bigger text, a chat overlay, private names hidden.
- **177. Practice undo.** Undo your last choice, in a practice mode only.
- **178. Benchmark mode.** Run 100 seasons without screens and show charts, for modders tuning balance.
- **179. Theme packs.** Colour schemes for the windows beyond grey.
- **180. Search everything.** One box to find any wrestler, match, show or news line.

- **182. Pin to the desk.** Pin a wrestler, a feud or a city to the desk.
- **183. Watch lists.** Be told when a free agent, a rival or a feud changes.
- **184. Bookmarks in history.** Mark moments to find again.
- **185. Remind me.** A reminder on any matter, for a week you choose.
- **186. Side by side.** Compare two wrestlers on one screen.
- **187. Draft cards.** Save a draft card for next month's big night and see it checked each week.
- **188. Gentle mode.** No firing, kinder crowds, for relaxed play.
