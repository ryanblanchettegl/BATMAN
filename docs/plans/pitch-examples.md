# What the writers would pitch: examples from the default world (draft for Ryan, 8 October)

These are written by hand to show what the feud engine should produce. The people, ratings, gimmicks, titles, hometowns and friendships are the real ones from the default world (WHW, week 1). Anything marked **backstory** is the kind of thing generated backstory would add; it is not in the game yet. Two calendar facts: WHW's big events are All Hallows' Eve (end of October), Gunpowder Plot (November) and Ragnarok (December, its flagship show of the year).

## What a pitch card shows

- **Title** and **shape**.
- **The hook**: one or two sentences, written from the strongest reason the engine found.
- **The cast and their roles.** Each role says what it needs, and the booker's read on whether this person has it. The read is in words and only as good as what the office has seen (fog of war).
- **Length and the night it ends.**
- **What the writers are hinting at**: the next big moment, never spelled out.
- **Why the writers picked it**: the facts it was built from, so the player learns how the engine thinks.

## Brief 1: Power, told in the building, dramatic, bold, long

### Pitch A: "The King's Court" (the rebel and the boss)
**Hook:** Henry VIII has been made general manager, and his first order is that the Empress Title answers to him. Elizabeth I, the champion, grew up in the same town and has never answered to anybody.

| Role | Who | Needs | The read |
|---|---|---|---|
| The boss | Henry VIII (rebel gimmick, villain, leads The Princes) | A great talker; somebody the crowd wants to see lose | "He can talk. We have heard him." |
| The rebel | Elizabeth I (rebel gimmick, hero, Empress champion) | Charisma; can carry a feud on the microphone | "Best talker in the building." |
| The boss's muscle | The Princes: Ivan the Terrible, Louis XIV, Robespierre | Ring work; a willingness to lose to her | "Ivan and Vlad have real heat. Keep them apart." |

**Length:** long, the road to Ragnarok. Chapter ends: All Hallows' Eve, Gunpowder Plot, then Ragnarok.
**The writers hint:** "The boss has a contract he wants her to sign. Watch the go-home show."
**Why:** both are from Greenwich (hometown); Henry leads a stable that can enforce; Elizabeth holds a title the boss can threaten; the brief asked for power and the building. Needs the authority figure role (Ryan, 8 October). Henry never has to wrestle her: the matches are her against his men, so the women's and men's divisions do not have to meet.

### Pitch B: "Two Emperors" (the chase)
**Hook:** Napoleon Bonaparte says Alexander the Great is champion only because they have never met. He is the better talker, and the better liar.

| Role | Who | Needs | The read |
|---|---|---|---|
| The champion | Alexander the Great (old hand, hero, Imperial World champion) | Ring work and charisma | "Best in the company." |
| The challenger | Napoleon Bonaparte (old hand, villain) | A great talker who can make the crowd hate him | "Born for it." |

**Length:** long, ending at Ragnarok with the title on the line.
**The writers hint:** "Napoleon does not want a fair fight. Somebody close to the champion is listening to him."
**Why:** the two best wrestlers in the company and only one belt; both old hands (the mirror pairing "who is the greatest"); both great on the microphone, so a story told on the microphone and in the building will land.

## Brief 2: Betrayal, told on the microphone, dramatic, safe, medium

### Pitch A: "Brothers in Arms" (the friend who turns)
**Hook:** Spartacus and Shaka Zulu are friends in the ring and out of it. The writers want Shaka to decide he has carried Spartacus long enough.

| Role | Who | Needs | The read |
|---|---|---|---|
| The one betrayed | Spartacus (old hand, hero) | Selling: the crowd has to feel it | "Not seen yet." |
| The one who turns | Shaka Zulu (old hand, hero, turns villain) | A believable turn: a talker who can explain why | "He has never been a villain here. Nobody knows if he can." |

**Length:** medium, ending at Gunpowder Plot.
**The writers hint:** "One of them has been offered something. Nobody has said by whom."
**Why:** the relationship matrix says they really are friends, so the betrayal is believable; they are close in age and level, so there is a reason to be jealous; the brief asked for betrayal. **Risk the pitch states:** neither has done a turn here, so it is a gamble on Selling.

### Pitch B: "The Teacher" (the friend who turns, the other way)
**Backstory:** Genghis Khan trained El Cid years ago, in another company.
**Hook:** El Cid owes Genghis everything, and Genghis has come to collect.

| Role | Who | Needs | The read |
|---|---|---|---|
| The student | El Cid (workhorse, hero) | Ring work; selling a beating | "Solid. Not much of a talker." |
| The teacher | Genghis Khan (outlaw, villain) | Charisma; menace | "The crowd boos him on sight." |

**Length:** medium, ending at All Hallows' Eve if started now (tight), or Gunpowder Plot.
**Why:** they are real friends in the matrix; generated backstory makes him the teacher; outlaw against workhorse is honour against no rules. **The pitch notes:** El Cid will need help on the microphone; told on the microphone, this one rests on Genghis.

## Brief 3: Respect, told in the ring, sporting, safe, short

### Pitch A: "The Sword and the Stone"
**Hook:** Miyamoto Musashi has never been beaten on the mat. Milo of Croton has never been knocked off his feet. One of them is about to lose something.

| Role | Who | Needs | The read |
|---|---|---|---|
| The champion | Miyamoto Musashi (technician, Iron Crown champion) | Ring work | "As good as anyone alive." |
| The challenger | Milo of Croton (clean-cut hero) | Ring work | "Strongest man here." |

**Length:** short, three weeks, a title match at All Hallows' Eve.
**Why:** technician against power is a clash of styles; both are heroes, so it is a respect feud; **neither can talk** (microphone 60 and 40), so the writers built it for the ring and kept it short. If the booker had asked for a story told on the microphone, the engine would not have pitched these two.

### Pitch B: "Pharaoh's Demand" (over the top, offered as the bold option)
**Hook:** Ramses the Great has decided the Magna Carta Television Title is his by divine right of pharaohs, and has told William Wallace so on television. Every week.

| Role | Who | Needs | The read |
|---|---|---|---|
| The comedy heel | Ramses the Great (comedy, villain) | A great talker, timing | "Funny. The crowd likes him more than they should." |
| The champion | William Wallace (brute, hero, television champion) | Charisma, a short temper | "Good on the microphone." |

**Why:** the brief said sporting, so this is the writers' wild card; comedy against brute is the joke that goes too far. **The pitch warns:** WHW's crowd wants it serious; an over-the-top feud is a gamble here.

## What happens once a pitch is picked

- The beats go on the run sheet, marked as the feud's.
- **Big moments are drawn from the shape's pool**, so "The King's Court" might, in one game, have the boss strip her title and, in another, have one of his own men refuse an order. Each has calls on the air that branch it ("the crowd is cheering the man who refused: make him a hero, or have the boss make an example of him").
- What happens writes back into history: the next time the engine looks at Henry VIII and Elizabeth I, it knows what happened between them, and so do they.

## Questions this draft raises

1. Is the authority figure allowed to feud across the men's and women's divisions, as long as the matches stay within one (Pitch 1A)?
2. Should a pitch say how bold it is with a risk word ("safe", "a gamble"), or with a percentage chance like other attempts in the game (rule 4)?
3. Is "Why the writers picked it" useful, or does it give away too much of how the engine works?
