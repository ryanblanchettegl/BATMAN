# How wrestling companies space their big events

Ryan, 15 October 2026: "I don't want two big events in one month. Most companies do 2 to 4 weeks of television and one PPV at the end of the month. Research how wrestling PPVs work throughout history." This note is research for the game; game content still never names a real company or event (rule 3).

## What the research found

**WWE.**
- The first pay-per-view was WrestleMania in 1985.
- Four shows became the yearly core: Royal Rumble, WrestleMania, SummerSlam and Survivor Series, called the Big Four.
- In the mid-1990s it went monthly: the In Your House series filled the months between the big four.
- After the 2002 brand split it peaked at sixteen events a year in 2006, then came back to twelve in 2012.
- Money in the Bank is now treated as a fifth big one, and the events are called premium live events.

The pattern: a big event every month, but four or five of them matter far more than the rest.

**TNA.**
- It began with weekly pay-per-views in 2002, then from November 2004 ran a monthly big event, with Bound for Glory in October as its biggest.
- In January 2013 it cut to four live events a year: Genesis, Lockdown, Slammiversary and Bound for Glory.
- The months between were filled with One Night Only, cheaper taped specials (2013 to 2019), then monthly streaming specials.

The pattern: a small company that could not fill a monthly big show went to four real ones and cheap taped nights in between.

**AEW.**
- AEW started in 2019 with four big events a year, about one a quarter: Revolution, Double or Nothing, All Out and Full Gear.
- All In was later added as a fifth.
- Between them sit smaller pay-per-views and specials (Dynasty, Forbidden Door, Redemption and others), with weekly television carrying the stories.

The pattern: a few marquee nights a year, and the months between are lighter.

**NJPW.**
- The year is built from tours of many small shows rather than a monthly event.
- It is anchored by a few fixtures:
  - Wrestle Kingdom at the Tokyo Dome on 4 January, the biggest night;
  - the New Japan Cup tournament in spring;
  - Dominion in early summer;
  - the G1 Climax, a month-long round-robin tournament in summer (about 19 shows) whose winner earns the title match at the next Wrestle Kingdom.

The pattern: tours and tournaments build to a handful of big nights, and a tournament can be the story of a whole month.

**Before cable pay-per-view** (from general history, not checked against a source in this search):
- Territories ran a few supercards a year, some on closed-circuit television.
- Weekly studio television existed to sell the house shows.
- The monthly big event is a 1990s habit, not a law of the business.

## What it means for the game

What the game does now:
- Every company runs two to four weeks of television and one big event in the last week of the month.
- That matches what Ryan asked for and how most companies have worked.
- A second big event in a month is dropped (NEXT.md 3.4).

What the research suggests: the companies differ in which months matter, not in how many events a month. A way to show that without breaking the one-a-month rhythm:

1. **Tentpoles.**
   - A company can mark up to four months as tentpoles besides its flagship.
   - A tentpole draws a bigger house and more buys, and the crowd expects more.
   - The ordinary months in between are smaller. This is the "big four" shape.
2. **Each company model starts with its own calendar:**
   - the corporate giant: a flagship and three tentpoles (monthly, four matter);
   - the diehards' company: four marquee nights and lighter months between;
   - the underdog: a flagship and three tentpoles, and taped specials in the quiet months (cheaper, smaller, less at stake);
   - the purist or the joshi company: a tournament month (the existing tournament night) that leads to a title match at the flagship.
3. **The flagship's story.** The winner of a company's tournament month earns the main event of its flagship, as the summer tournament does.

None of this needs a second event in a month. It uses the kinds of night that already exist (flagship, arena, ballroom, gimmick, battle royal, tournament, dark), adds tentpole and taped special, and gives each model its own starting year.
