# TEW IX deep dive — Volume 3: companies, money, backstage

Handbook chapters 8, 16, 17, 18. `[hb §]` throughout.

## Sizes and hidden importance

Company size drives payroll expectations, brand allowances, show costs, bankruptcy leeway,
downside norms and which deals the AI will offer.

**Importance** is hidden and derived from **Size and Prestige** — a Small company with 0
prestige still outranks a Tiny company with 100 — plus investment boosts. Importance sets
the **order companies run shows each night**, and the first to run gets first choice of
shared workers, venues and local hires. `[hb 16.2]`

**Ranking score** = `Σ (popularity in region × that region's potential fan base)`. A company
with 50% in Great Lakes (fan base 90%) scores 45 there. `[hb 16.3]`

## Finance

- **Misc** is a balancing catch-all tied to size — very profitable companies get hit with
  large misc costs. `[hb 16.8]`
- **Tax** only in profitable months, **5%–40%** by profit size. `[hb 16.8]`
- **Bankruptcy** depends on **consecutive months in debt**, not the amount: **6 months to 3
  years** by size, extended for companies older than a decade. All companies get a **3-year
  grace period** after opening. Child companies cannot go bankrupt. `[hb 16.8]`
- Cash figures cap at **±$999,999,999**. `[hb 41.12]`

## Merchandising — 10 levels

Revenue is **net** of costs, and includes home video/DVD/VoD distribution by era. `[hb 16.19]`

| Level | Monthly cost | Per fan (live) | Per popularity point (mail order) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Ramshackle | $0 | $0.20 | $0 |
| 2 Amateur | $0 | $0.50 | $1 |
| 3 Small Time | $0 | $1 | $3 |
| 4 Growing Business | $750 | $2.50 | $5 |
| 5 Comprehensive | $1,250 | $3 | $10 |
| 6 Professional | $2,500 | $5 | $15 |
| 7 Industrial Scale | $5,000 | $7.50 | $20 |
| 8 Enviable | $10,000 | $12 | $30 |
| 9 Award Winning | $25,000 | $15 | $40 |
| 10 World Class | $50,000 | $25 | $50 |

Worked example: level 5 with 100 attendees takes **$300** live, and **30 popularity in London**
yields **~$300/month** regardless of shows there. Economy and audience boredom modify both.
Upgrades take increasing time and money — deliberately slow to reach level 10. `[hb 16.19]`

**Figurehead** effect: up to **+20% attendance** (only on shows he appears on) and up to
**+75% merchandise** (if he has appeared in the last **two months**). Figureheads in good
standing also trend to high morale; keeping an ineligible one makes them a liability and
removing one causes a major morale hit. `[hb 16.14]`

## Production ladders — exact costs

All five ladders price a level and penalise being under-levelled. `[hb 8.39–8.44]`

**Production Values**
| Level | Cost |
|---|---|
| Limited | $150/mo |
| Amateur | $500/mo + $150/show |
| Semi-Pro | $1,500/mo + $500/show |
| Professional | $7,500/mo + $1,500/show |
| High Quality | $15,000/mo + $10,000/show |
| Majestic | $50,000/mo + $75,000/show |
| Industry Leader | $100,000/mo + $125,000/show |

**Live Event Experience** — Volunteers (free) · Small ($2,000/show) · Medium ($5,000/show) ·
Large ($15,000/show) · Professional ($100,000/mo + $25,000/show).

**Broadcast Quality** — Amateur ($100/show) · Semi-Pro ($500/show) · Hired Pro ($2,500/show) ·
In House ($7,500/mo) · Industry Standard ($75,000/mo) · Cutting Edge ($150,000/mo).

**Music** — Small Time (free) · Generic ($100/show) · Local ($250/show) · In House
($10,000/mo) · Minor Licensed ($25,000/mo) · Licensed ($50,000/mo) · Major Licensed
($100,000/mo).

**Penalty scale**: Production Values and Broadcast Quality **−3%** (one level short) to
**−7%** (three or more short); Live Event Experience and Music **−2% to −6%**. Total across
all areas is **capped at −10%** of the final grade. `[hb 8.41]`

## Products

Product "controls virtually everything": what the audience expects, match/angle weighting,
advertiser appeal, gimmick fit and in-ring tolerances. Changing it takes time and can only go
to a **related** product. `[hb 17.1]`

Eleven settings: Current Product · Core Product · Gender Setting · Match Focus · Angle
Focus · Face/Heel Divide · Men's Weight Split · Minis Divisions · Stables · Managers ·
In-Ring Settings.

Product also supplies the **short-match caps** (e.g. "matches under 20 minutes are capped",
19 min → 90, 14 min → 80). `[hb 17.11, 10.8]`

## Storylines

- A segment counts for a storyline if it contains **≥2 workers from it, at least one in a
  Major role, and they aren't all aligned**. `[hb 16.13]`
- It moves heat only if its rating is **±5** of current heat (first segment sets opening
  heat). `[hb 16.13]`
- Minimums by size, or penalties apply `[hb 8.13]`:

| Size | Hot at heat | Active storylines | Furthered per show |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insignificant | 15 | 1 | 1 |
| Tiny | 20 | 1 | 1 |
| Small | 35 | 2 | 1 |
| Medium | 50 | 2 | 1 |
| Big | 65 | 3 | 2 |
| Huge / Titanic | 70 | 3 | 2 |

## Brands and divisions

Six brand types `[hb 16.11]`:

| Brand | Size | Allowed perception |
|---|---|---|
| Major | no limit | any, Major Star down |
| Showcase | ~20 | any |
| Niche | ~15 | any except Major Stars |
| Minor | ~20 | no Major Stars or Stars |
| Reserve | no limit | Recognisable and Unimportant only |
| Throwaway | ~15 | Unimportant only |

AI drafts: a **Complete Draft** when >10% of the roster is unbranded, and roughly annually to
refresh. `[hb 16.11]`

**Divisions**: up to **10** custom names, purely organisational; workers bind to the slot, so
renaming keeps membership. `[hb 16.26]`

## Backstage

**Locker-room morale** = three sections added: **notable personalities + relationships +
other factors**. Only relationships are **capped at ±25%**; the other two are uncapped.
A worker's **Perception** scales their influence — a bad-tempered main eventer hurts far more
than a midcarder. `[hb 16.9]`

The office rating assumes **everyone is present**; the show-time figure can differ
drastically when people are absent. `[hb 8.25]`

**Backstage rules** (7) generate or suppress morale incidents: no drugs/alcohol/smoking ·
arriving and leaving times · dress code · no outsiders · catering/masseuse/chiropractor ·
transport and hotels · dirty doctor. `[hb 18]`

**Dirty doctor**: steroid-enhanced workers gain physical skills faster, decline slower and
gain Star Power quicker — at the cost of drug-related negatives and a **major scandal risk**
if a worker dies, overdoses or is caught. `[hb 18.7]`

**Drug policy** and **scandals** are separate from rules: policy sets testing, scandals are
discrete public incidents.

## Child companies

Parent owns the roster, may alter schedules and belts. A child that **accepts developmental
workers** cannot sign independently; the parent maintains the roster. Finances are absorbed
monthly to **$0**, so a child cannot go bankrupt unless the parent does. `[hb 30.4, 30.14]`

## Touring, young lions, headquarters

- **Touring company**: has ≥1 event with **Tour** intent; workers get **25% pay** on tour
  shows; not broadcast (taped only for highlights). `[hb 16.40, 24.15]`
- **Young lions**: **<25 Experience** and debuted **<3 years** ago. `[hb 16.39]`
- **Corporate HQ**: one-off **+10% prestige**, ongoing **+25% influence**. `[hb 16.41]`
- **Physical Hall of Fame**: one-off **+5% prestige**, ongoing **+15% influence**. `[hb 16.42]`

## Owner goals and promises

Goals are typed tasks with criteria (e.g. "has won primary titles" — floating titles at **≥70
prestige** count; "big events" = Normal intent, non-weekly). Owner **patience**, **roster
turnover** preference and **finance** setting shape how hard the goals are and how fast
failure escalates. `[hb 16.22, 21.7]`

## Coaching notes for a rebuild

1. **Two hidden corporate scores** (importance, influence) set show order; do not model order
   as player choice alone.
2. **Costs are ladders, not linear** — the top rungs are 100× the bottom.
3. **Penalties are capped** (10% for production gaps) — stack smaller modifiers freely.
4. **Merchandise is net revenue** and mostly mail-order driven by popularity points.
5. **Locker room is an aggregate of three uncapped/capped sections** — reproduce the ±25%
   cap on relationships or the room becomes purely social.
