# EWF Wrestling Manager: working notes for Claude Code

EWF Wrestling Manager (in full, Elite Wrestling Federation Wrestling Manager) is a text-based wrestling booking sim for Steam, drawn as a 1990s PC program. Ryan renamed it on 7 October 2026; it was EWF 9000 before, and the stored keys keep that name on purpose (rule 9). The owner is Ryan. `NEXT.md` is the work queue: ordered steps for the next unattended run. `TASKS.md` is the task list and holds his decisions; `TWEAKS.md` is 100 small jobs and `WISHLIST.md` is 100 depth ideas, each with its own "how to work this list"; `docs/feature-list.md` is the full feature wording; `docs/design.md` is the design document; `app/DEV.md` says how screens are written.

Each test prints its failures and exits non-zero if any. They are slow (one to five minutes each); run the ones for the section you touched, then `journey.js`.

## How the code is laid out

- `src/*.js`: the simulation. Plain ES5 in one shared closure, concatenated in file-name order by `build.js`. No screen code. State `S` is plain JSON and is the save file. A seeded generator (`rnd(S)`) makes every game repeatable. Systems plug in through hook lists declared at the top of `src/10-match.js` (`MQX`, `CRX`, `FINX`, `EFX`, `POST`, `SHOWX`, `WEEKX`, `NEWX`, `PREX`, `TASKX`).
- `src/66-relations.js`: the relationship matrix (`S.rm`) and what people remember (`S.rmY` for what they remember about the booker). It is the one place that says how two people feel about each other. Change it only through `relBump()` and `youRemember()`; read it with `relOf()`, `bondOf()`, `respOf()`, `jealOf()`. It never calls `rnd(S)`. `note(S, heading, line, kind)` puts a line on the notification bar. `docs/plans/gorilla-position.md` is the plan this belongs to.
- `src/30-show.js`, `src/31-live.js` and `app/src/screens/booking/Live.tsx`: the live show. The player's show is not worked out in one go. `E.liveBegin(S, card)` puts it on the air (`S.live`, plain data, so a save taken mid-show loads back into it), `E.liveNext(S)` runs the next promo, angle or match, and the show stops whenever the gorilla position needs a call (`{event}`) until `E.liveDecide(S, choice)` answers it. A kind of call is one entry in `LIVEK` (`pre` or `post` offers it from what is true of the next step, never a flat chance; `run` applies the answer through `relBump()`, `youRemember()`, feuds and titles). `livePlay()` runs a whole show with the safe answers, and `E.runPlayerShow` is that. Rival shows run straight through with no calls. The broadcast screen is one screen: what is on the air on the left, tonight's run sheet on the right. A show on the air cannot be skipped or left. The SPACE key at the right end of the bottom line is the only way forward: it says what it will do (Ring bell, Continue, Next, Sign off, Report, Your call) and holds the focus (`liveStep()` and `liveGo()` in `screens/booking/run.ts`). The broadcast has no continue button of its own. A promo, angle or match can be skipped to its result; a call from the gorilla position can never be skipped. Auto-play (`pref.auto`, on by default, P or the `live-auto` button; `autoArm()` in `run.ts`; pace `pref.pace` is full, quick or results, the `live-pace` button and Options) moves the broadcast on at a reading pace, lingers on a big moment (a beat with `h`: 1 for a near fall or a finish, 2 for a new champion, set in `callMatch()`), and never rings the opening bell, answers a call or leaves the sign-off. Browser tests run with it off unless `open({ auto: true })`; `app/tests/auto.js` tests it.
- `src/34-night.js` and `app/src/screens/booking/After.tsx`: After the show, where a show ends. The sign-off does not lead to the match-by-match report. It leads to the After the show screen (`book().night`; `liveDone()`, `openNight()`, `closeNight()`, `nightFull()` in `run.ts`): the grade and the verdict, the matter the night left that needs an answer (it can be answered there), and a list of what else it left with the picked one shown beside it. The report is one press away (Match by match) and closes back to this screen. The SPACE key says Office: on to the desk, where Before the show is the first thing. The Office has no After the show panel; it has one button, After the show recap, that comes back. When a show goes on the air the engine notes how everybody stood (`S.live.n0`), and when it goes off the difference is filed as `rep.left`: the gate, television, who got hurt, whose mood moved, what the notification bar said, what the writers thought. `E.afterShow(S)` lays it out, trouble first. A new thing a show should leave behind is one more `add()` in `E.afterShow`. A show can also leave one matter in the inbox that has to be answered (`nightMatter()`: a hurt champion, somebody furious, a lost belt and the rematch, an act that caught fire, an upset nobody called, a main event that was the weakest match, the match of the night with no story); a new one is one more case there and an `EVR` entry.
- `src/32-direction.js`: the calls that set the direction of the company. The owner on the headset (the office wants its favourite to win: do as told, refuse, or find a way round). Who the show is built around (`S.fc = {id, w}`, decided on the air after a main event; read it with `faceOf(S)` or `E.faceInfo(S)`). The crowd comes to see the face of the company: a lift to the show's hype when they are in the main event, a letdown when they could be on the card and are not (`faceHype()`, and a warning from `E.validate`), and louder matches. The network on the line (what is going out is more than the slot was sold as; the answers move the network's patience, `S.clocks.net`). The sponsor at ringside (a deal with four weeks or fewer left wants its name on the main event). A new call of this kind is one more entry in `LIVEK` here, added to `LIVE_ORDER` (before a segment) or `LIVE_POST` (after one).
- `src/33-ring.js`: three calls caused by the match in the ring. Somebody is hurt (a worn body in a demanding match; the answers are the old headset's bad landing, through `E.resolveChaos`). The crowd has gone quiet (the match before fell flat: stay, take it to the floor, the villain cheats, or call the upset). They are not going home (two who work well together, a hot feud or creative control: take it home, or give them the time and cut a segment, shorten the main event or run over the slot). The order every kind is tried in is `LIVE_ORDER`, set at the end of this file.
- `src/79-people.js` and `app/src/screens/office/Backstage.tsx`: Backstage is its own page in the Office (the desk has one line about it and a button), built as the board (`docs/mockups/final/final-2-backstage.png`, Ryan's pick): one screen of five windows. Who needs you tonight (everybody in the building for a reason, a line each with a face, a mark, mood and where they stand with you; worst first, by room or your door first; as many lines as the window holds, then pages), the file of whoever is picked down the right (`DossierBody`, which says less, one step at a time, when its window is short), Things to do (a few buttons a page, each opening its pop-up), At your door (requests answered there, free) and The room. It is people, not rooms. Seven rooms: Your office first (the old production truck, id `truck`: people come to the booker's door about their story), catering, the trainer's room, the gym, the parking lot, the owner's office, wrestlers' court. Nobody is in a room by chance: `peopleNow(S)` puts each person in the room their reason belongs to (hurt or worn down in the trainer's room; furious, low, real heat or the owner's favourite in catering; a rival's offer, a contract running down or an ambush in the parking lot; the breakout pick, an act the crowd is with or two rivals who do not click in the gym; a rivalry's next chapter, the champion, a newcomer or the face of the company in the truck). It only reads the game. One action point is spent on a person, once a week each (`E.peopleDo`, `S.apWho`), and what it does is an entry in `PPL_ACT`, through `relBump()` and `youRemember()`. A matter the last show left on the desk can be answered there (sitting down with somebody furious closes it). A new reason is one more block in `peopleNow()`. People also come to Your office to ask for something (kinds starting `ask`: a title shot, out of a rivalry, a turn, a team): the answers are free (`free: 1`), yes does the real thing, no and never being seen are remembered, and nobody asks again for twelve weeks (`S.lcd['ak' + id]`). Seven kinds so far: a title shot, out of a rivalry, a turn, a team, more money, two weeks at home, and the ending of a ripe story on the next big night (`askend`, which takes the place of the next-chapter visit). A scene is two people in a room together (`duo`; the screen names both): a team arguing at your door (`argue`), somebody jealous of another's spot (`jealous`), a veteran working late with a young one (`late`). A new scene is one more block in `peopleNow()` that marks both people as seen. The rooms' own actions (the owner, the court, the class, the pep talk, the truck) are still under "Around the building", one visit to each a week. Each person has a file (`src/79-dossier.js`, `E.dossier(S, id)`; the pop-up is `DossierWindow` in `screens/office/Dossier.tsx`, opened by Their file): why they are in the building, mood and how it moved since the last show (`S.moodAt`, written when your show goes off the air), where they stand with you, stress, the last three things they remember about you, two closest friends and two worst rivals, their stories, and the answers open to you with chance and cost. `E.roomInfo(S)` is the room in one line at the top of Backstage (trust, mood, unhappy, hurt, circles, the worst heat).
- `src/76-friends.js`: a friend of the champion becomes the next in line for their title (`friends`, once a pair, twelve weeks apart): a title match done with respect, let it turn sour (a feud), or keep them apart.
- `src/76-student.js`: when somebody a wrestler trained (`w.coach`) becomes more over than them, it reaches the desk once (`passed`): the teacher puts the student over, leave it (the teacher grows jealous), or teacher against student.
- `src/21-living.js`: living storylines (`docs/plans/living-storylines.md`, Ryan 14 October; it replaced the short, medium and long feuds). A feud has no length and no planned week: it has heat (how much the crowd cares) and freshness (`f.fresh`, how much it still has to give). Every beat raises heat and costs freshness, the same kind of beat again is worth less (`feudHeatRoom()`), and a twist or a turn or a week of rest gives freshness back. One-sided feuds and people in two feuds heat slower; real heat in the relationship matrix heats faster. It ends when the booker ends it: a match marked End the story here (`m.end`, `E.setMatchEnd`) or an ending planned for a night (`f.endAt`, `E.storyPlanEnd`); `feudDue()` says 'end' then, and 'chapter' for a round on a big night (`closeChapter()`). Suggest a card marks the ending of a ripe feud (hot and nearly used up) on a big night. The ending pays by the moment (`feudPayMult()`: heat, freshness and how long it lived). A feud that never gets going, stays cold for four weeks, or is left alone ten weeks fizzles (`feudFizzle()`). `E.feudPlan(S, f)` is everything the screens show (phase in words: Spark, Building, Boiling, Going stale, Cooling); `E.season(S)` is the heat line of every feud over the last twelve weeks and what the state says. Start a feud costs 2 booking power and asks only for two people (`E.storyPreview`, `E.storyStart`). Rival companies do not keep feuds yet; `rivalRipe()` is ready for when they do.
- `src/98-plot.js` and `Plot` in `app/src/screens/stories/Storylines.tsx`: booking power spent on the Storylines page. Three entries in `PLOT`: break up a tag team (2), push a wrestler (2, once in eight weeks each), buy a budget pre-tape (1). A pre-tape is an ordinary segment on the next show's run sheet marked `tape: 1`, placed at the start, the middle or the end, and a suggested card keeps it. `E.plotInfo(S)`, `E.plotDo(S, id, o)`. A new thing booking power buys there is one more entry.
- The rooms' own actions backstage (`E.apDo`) are buttons under "Things to do" on the Backstage page. Each opens a pop-up (`ActWindow`, modal kind `apact`) that says what it does, asks who, and spends the point. There is no menu of rooms.
- `src/78-models.js`: the nine company models. Each is data plus small functions for match quality, crowd, finishes, pushes, hiring fit and the suggested card.
- `app/src/`: the interface in Preact and TypeScript. `store.ts` holds the game and view state, `nav.ts` the screen map, `input.ts` screen modes and remote or gamepad focus, `kit/` the building blocks, `screens/<section>/` one folder per section, `shared/` pieces used by more than one section.
- `src/86-editor.js` and `app/src/screens/editor/`: the World Editor, opened from the title screen. It edits a universe package (plain JSON kept with the player's other worlds), never a running game. Every change goes through an `E.ed*` function so it can be tested headless.
- `src/87-create.js` and `app/src/screens/manage/Create.tsx`: belts and weekly shows made during a game. These are big moments, not yearly habits, and they follow real-life precedent: each takes an occasion (`beltOccasions()`, `showOccasions()`), a company adds one belt a year and one show in two at most, and rivals add them rarely, one at a time, only for something that has come about since the game began (`rivalGrow()`, once a year in each rival's own month; `S.mkw` spaces them across the world). `P.mk` is where a company stood, `P.grown` its record. A new occasion is one more entry in those two functions. A new show scales the company's overheads the way `calibrateCosts()` did for the shows it started with, and copies the main show's crowd expectations.
- `src/42-events.js`: big events belong to a company. `P.events` is twelve `{n, t, rule}` (type `flag` for the flagship, which gets an original name from `FLAG_WORDS`), filled from the world's list the first time `evList()` is called, so old saves need nothing. Name a big night with `evName(S, P, month)`, never `dbOf(S).events`. `E.events(S)` is the player's twelve. `EV_TYPES` is what each kind of night changes (house, price, buys, production, what the crowd expects; flagship and arena change nothing), read with `evTy(show)`; `E.evSet(S, m, {n, t})` renames or retypes a later month (the Year planner on Manage, Operations). One big event a month, always (Ryan, 15 October); `MODEL_YEAR` gives each kind of company its own starting year (tentpoles, taped specials, a tournament month whose winner is promised the flagship's title match), from `docs/research/big-event-calendars.md`.
- `src/97-clauses.js`: contract clauses (`docs/plans/contract-clauses.md`). `CLAUSE` holds paid travel, a no-cut promise, two dates a week, outside dates (indie work on off nights), a merchandise share and a title match; a wrestler wants at most three (`clausesWanted()`), each lowers what they ask, and they are kept on `w.cl` for the deal. `E.clauses(S, id)` lists them for the Offer window; `E.sign(S, id, wage, weeks, cl)` signs with them. A new clause is one more entry with `wants`, `save` and its effect.
- `src/88-shows.js`: a weekly show lasts. Every show of every company carries its years on the air (`sh.on`, `sh.ep`), a standing from 0 to 100 that is only ever said in words (`sh.pr`, `airWord()`: Brand new, Finding its feet, Established, A fixture, Appointment viewing, An institution), a network and its size (`sh.net`, `sh.tier`) and the week its deal is up (`sh.deal`). Standing moves a little with every episode (beating what the crowd expected, a growing audience, a point on each anniversary) and brings viewers, measured against where the show began (`airPF()`), so a new game starts where the calibration put it. When a deal is up the player answers it in the inbox (`rights`: stay, move, hold out) and a rival settles its own. A show is never cancelled by a rival: it moves to a smaller network (`airDemote()`). An anniversary or a milestone episode is an occasion (`airNight()`). Nothing here locks a new show or belt in a booker's first years.
- `src/89-networks.js`: what a network wants (NEXT.md 7.1, 7.3). Each network has a taste from its name (`netTaste()`: family, sport, late night, niche; `NET_TASTE`) and each deal a ratings target (`sh.tgt`, what the show drew when it was signed). Every episode of yours is measured against both (`rep.net`): a clash with the taste costs the network's patience every second time, pleasing it or four hits in a row gives some back, three misses in a row and an executive calls (`netexec`). At renewal a show rated a fixture or better can start a bidding war (a fourth answer on `rights`). `E.netInfo(S)` is what the screens show. It wraps `airSign`, `airAsk` and `EVR.rights` from `src/88-shows.js`, so it sorts after it. No `rnd(S)` outside the answers' attempts.
- `src/89-streaming.js`: your own streaming service (NEXT.md 7.4; Manage, Deals, Streaming). It costs about a week and a half of income to open and a little each week to run; subscribers pay every week and grow toward a ceiling set by the company's size, its popularity and the tape library (`streamCap()`); good shows bring people in, bad ones lose some. While it runs, cities you do not tour do not cool (`streamOn()` in the market cooling in `src/91-road.js`), and a show on a major network is paid a little less (it wraps `airRate`). `P.strm`, `E.streamInfo`, `E.streamStart`, `E.streamStop`. No `rnd(S)`.
- `src/81-invasion.js`: invasions (WISHLIST 66). A war with a rival (`S.xf`, `src/80-world.js`) starts with a call about the finishes (`xfdeal`): trade wins (both look strong), ask them to put your people over (an attempt; refused, they pull out), or let the ring decide. A war lost by two or more leaves the loser weak for a year (`P.weak`; less hype on your big nights, a popularity hit for a rival), `E.weakUntil`. It wraps `startXf` and `endXf`, so it sorts after `src/80-world.js`.
- `src/88-nights.js`: nights. A weekly show's night comes from its name (`nightOf()`, Spanish names too). A rival in the same part of the world (`regionOf()`, the road's map) with a weekly show on the same night goes head to head with yours: tonight's audience against theirs, the nearest in size of several. Winning the night adds a little hype; `rep.h2h` is the result, `S.nw[day]` the count, and After the show and the news say so. Once a year at most the network offers to move a show onto a rival's night (`nightmove`; `sh.day` overrides the name). No `rnd(S)`.
- `src/92-shape.js` and `app/src/screens/booking/Shape.tsx`: the running order. Where a match sits on the card changes how it lands (opener, back-to-back, main event, finishes across the night). `E.shape(S, card)` reads a card before the show; `shapeAuto()` lays out automatic cards by the same rules.
- `src/93-segments.js` and `app/src/screens/booking/Segments.tsx`: promos and angles the player books on the run sheet. There is no fixed number of them. Each is short, medium or long (5, 10 or 15 minutes). The kind `writers` hands that time to the writers (`genAngle`); the writers add nothing on their own.
- `src/97-time.js`: time is the budget. A show is two hours of weekly television or three of big event, and the run sheet has to fill it (`E.clock(S)`, `E.matchMins()`, `E.fitShow()`). More than five minutes over, or more than ten empty, and the show cannot run. The first thing on the sheet opens the show, and each way of opening pays in its own way; whatever is on the air at the top of each hour is judged against what the crowd expects. `E.suggest(S)` returns a whole show that fits, and sets the promos and angles as it goes. Anything that books without a person must call `E.fitShow(S, card)` after changing a card. Only the player's shows are on the clock.
- `src/99-fog.js`: fog of war. The game does not say how a match or a promo will go. The road agent comments on every match on the sheet (`E.agentReads(S)`), and only says what can be seen, what the office has seen on its own shows (`S.know`: pairings, who blows up, who has been heard on the microphone), or what the agent can read. A better agent, an agent put on the match, and the booker's Eye for talent and level all make a read more likely. A read is never wrong; a weaker agent just says less. Promos and angles get a read in words (`look.read`), not a forecast in stars. During the broadcast the agent speaks in the booker's ear (beats of type `ear`), and the report lists what was learned (`rep.learned`). A new thing the player should have to learn goes here: hide it before the bell, name it in the ear, and add it to what the night teaches.
- `src/60-grammar.js`: the text grammar (Tracery in spirit, with Improv's rule that a line is used only when it is true of the game). `gram(R, start, ctx, key)` expands `#sym#` and fills `{key}`; a choice is a string or `{t, if, w}`. Every pick comes from `h01()`, never `rnd(S)`. Rule sets: `GR_SHEET` (the newsletter's headline, opening and verdict), `GR_WEB` (each Wrestle Search site's headline, `E.webHeads`), `GR_CHAT` (the chat room after a show), `GR_PROMO` (what a wrestler says in a booked promo, `promoSpeech()`: opener, a callback to real history, the point, a closer; the mic decides polished, plain or fumbled). New lines are new choices in a rule set; `test-grammar.js` checks them.
- `src/84-sift.js`: story sifting (in the manner of Felt). `E.sift(S)` looks through head to head records (`S.h2h`), title changes, the relationship matrix, who trained whom and tag teams for pairs on your roster who already have a story and are not feuding: the decider, the rematch never given, a loss to pay back, real heat, teacher and student, partners apart. Each has its facts and a pitch from `GR_SIFT`. Storylines shows them under Stories waiting, and Start this feud opens the story pop-up with both picked. A new pattern is one more block that calls `add()`.
- `src/94-net.js` and `app/src/screens/net/`: the Net section (dirt sheet, the feed, the boards). It reads the game and never uses `rnd(S)`; lines are picked with `h01()`, so a post can never change how a game plays out.
- `src/95-tasks.js` and `app/src/screens/office/Tasks.tsx`: this week's tasks on the desk. ## Rules

1. **The engine owns the game.** A screen never writes to `S`. It calls `E.something(S, ...)` inside `act()` and redraws.
2. **Zero external assets.** No hosted images, fonts, scripts or styles. Borders, bevels and scanlines are CSS; charts and room art are text; portraits are drawn on a canvas by code; the fonts are embedded as base64.
2a. **Read `docs/plans/art-direction.md` before any work on the look.** It is the plan for the new art direction: the kit, company marks, lights, the engine work each screen needs, the build order, and the questions still open with Ryan. **The look is grey desktop windows on the blue desktop** (Ryan, 6 October: "the new art direction of the game"). `docs/mockups/final/` is the reference set (the desk, Backstage, Storylines, the show with its pop-ups, Roster, Book the show): a grey window with a blue title bar and a drop shadow, grouped boxes with a red heading, green buttons with a hard black shadow, black text on grey. The window kit, the bottom line with its SPACE key, and the lights are built, pop-ups wear the look, and the desk is the first page built from it (`TASKS.md`, section 0p). Build new pages toward it and move old ones one at a time. No black backgrounds for new panels, with two exceptions he chose: the broadcast keeps its black scrolling text, and dot-matrix pictures are colour lights on a black board set inside a grey window (`docs/mockups/dots-sheet1.png`; built as `LightPic` and `LightText` in `app/src/kit/lights.tsx`, painted by code on a small grid, so they are code, not image files).
3. **Original content only.** Models and storylines may follow how real companies and angles worked. Game content never names a real promotion, wrestler, stable, title or event. The default universe is public domain: people who died before 1926, myth and folklore, or fiction published before 1929 by an author who died before 1955. No figures still honoured in a living religion. No real-world rosters in this repository. Exception (Ryan, 16 October, on his lawyer's advice): a parody universe sits beside the public-domain one. It parodies real wrestlers and companies under changed, joking names, never the real name or a near-copy, with no real logos or photos, and the game shows a parody disclaimer.
4. **No dice wording.** Checks are "attempts" with a percentage chance and a list of what helps and what hurts.
5. **Four inputs.** Every control is a real button, select or input, reachable by mouse, touch, keyboard and remote or gamepad. Nothing depends on hover. Give each control a `data-t` name; the tests find controls that way.
5. (continued) **No phone, no portrait.** The game is for a wide screen: desk, tablet and TV. There is no phone layout and no phone test run. A tall narrow window shows one line asking for a wide one. Do not add narrow-screen layout rules.
5a. **Where things sit.** Top: the menu bar, with the company and the date at its right end and nothing laid over it, and the notification bar under it. Bottom: one line. The numbers and how they moved on the left, then three small keys (Help, Options, Music), then the SPACE key at the right end, which says what happens next. There is no other big button. Tasks live on the desk, nowhere else. Nothing else goes in those bars. The bottom line never wraps: check it at 1280 wide, on a tablet and on a TV when anything is added to it.
5b. **One screen, no scrolling.** Ryan wants every page to fit the window with nothing to scroll. A window may scroll inside itself where it holds a stream of reading (Web, Ryan 14 October: "we can use more scrolling tools"); the page itself never scrolls, and `fits()` lets a window marked `overflow-y:auto` through. A page that has been converted wraps itself in `.onescreen` (styles at the end of `app/styles/booking.css`): the frame becomes exactly one window tall and the page's parts share the height. On a one-screen page type is sized from the window's height as well as its width (`--fs` in `base.css`, the `:has(.onescreen)` rules), so about 29 lines always fit; pages that still scroll keep the old sizes until they are converted. The pattern is a compact list on the left, one line an item, and a pane on the right that shows whatever is selected, or a tab of notes when nothing is. A list that can outgrow its box turns pages (`Paged` in `screens/booking/Side.tsx`); it never scrolls. Test with `fits(page)` from `app/tests/helper.js` and `app/tests/onescreen.js`, at 1280x720 as well as larger. Converted so far: the card builder, the broadcast, After the show, the desk, Backstage, Storylines, Career, Roster, Locker room, Titles, Free agents, Web, History (each as a page of windows; History is the books in a list and the picked one beside it), Overview (the gauges, then its parts in a list and the picked one beside it), Finances (the money, the weekly net, and the books by tab), and World (as `.onescreen.winpage`: the old panels share the height and scroll inside themselves). The Manage pages are short tile pages that already fit. Do not add a page, a panel or a block of text to a converted page without checking it still fits. Pages not yet converted still scroll; convert them one at a time, with Ryan's go-ahead for each.
6. **Six sections:** Office (The desk, Backstage, Storylines, Career), Booking, Roster (Roster, Locker room, Titles, Free agents), Web (one page, Ryan 14 October: the dirt sheet as a 90s newsletter, the feed as a 90s chat room, and Wrestle Search, six make-believe wrestling sites in `app/src/screens/net/Search.tsx` with the fan board among them; a new site is one entry in `SITES`), Manage (Operations, House, Deals), Company (Overview, Finances, World, History). The desk, Backstage and Storylines are the main tabs of the Office (Ryan, 6 October); there is no Stories section any more. Web is information only, like Company. The feed scrolls in its window, and the dirt sheet is drawn as a 90s newsletter on paper (Ryan, 14 October). Manage holds every choice; Company is information only; the desk opens on the road, with what needs an answer under it.
6a. **Choices are buttons that open pop-ups.** Ryan, 6 October: a page of choices is a page of buttons. Each button says what it is and what you get (or where it stands now), and pressing it opens a pop-up with the choices. A result closes back to the pop-up it came from (`BACK_TO` in `store.ts`: Manage, Storylines, Backstage). Done so far: Backstage ("Things to do", `ACT_UI` in `screens/office/BeforeShow.tsx` names each button by what it does), Storylines (`Plot`, `PlotWindow`), and all three Manage pages (`tiles()` and `ManageWindow` in `screens/manage/Operations.tsx`: a tile is an id, a name, what it is set to now, and the body of its pop-up). The buttons share the `.bsact` style. A browser test that wants the choices themselves laid out on the page sets `window.EWF_FLAT = 1` (see `app/tests/company.js`); `ensure(page, sel)` in the helper opens whichever button holds a control.
7. **Readable first.** Ryan's design goal is the pull of "one more turn" with screens that are easy to read and quick to act on. Every action shows its result at once. Prefer a pop-up or a short note to another panel.
7a. **No clean stopping point.** Office, Booking and the Show each hand the next one something unresolved (`docs/design.md`, section 2a, in Ryan's words). The week opens on a fire with a deadline. Booking is a risk and reward puzzle where chemistry, morale and fatigue are limits the player can see. The Show pays out and ends on a new problem or chance for next week. A feature that ends a phase on a clean slate is not finished: say what it hands to the next phase.
8. **Plain writing in the game and in docs.** Short sentences, no jargon, no em dashes in new text.
8a. **Stars, not percentages.** Stars are for after the bell: nothing is forecast in stars before a show (see `src/99-fog.js`). How good a match, promo or angle was is shown as stars (`stars()` in the kit, `starG()` in the engine), never as a number. A target about a match is worded in stars and checked with `starMeets()`. Chances stay percentages (rule 4).
8c. **A show is a letter grade and a word.** The letter is the show against its own crowd (Ryan, 6 October): an A beat what this crowd expected, a B is what they came for, and it is never a percentage. `crowdScore(rating, exp)` in `src/98-words.js` puts the gap on the grade scale; a filed show carries it as `rep.cs` and is read with `repCS(rep)`, `E.repGrade(rep)` on a screen. Never grade `rep.rating` directly. A target about a show is worded as a grade on that scale and checked with `gradeMeets()`. How good a show was, whoever it was for, and what a crowd expects are said in words on the ladder (a bomb, a dud, flat, decent, solid, strong, hot, red hot, blow-away, all-time classic): `E.expectWords(S)`, `E.showWord(v)`. How a show did against its crowd is `E.showVerdict(rating, exp)`: a headline, one line, and a band (`verdictBand()`) that the letter, the dirt sheet and the Net all follow, so they cannot disagree. New text about a show uses these, not a made-up phrase.
8b. **Money:** short form (`cash()`, `$1.2M`) in tables and lists; full form (`full()`, `$1,200,000`) for a single headline figure.
9. **Saves:** the save is `S` under the key `ewf9000-save-4`. A change that breaks old saves needs a version bump and a migration.
9a. **One step at a time.** Do one step, show Ryan the result, and wait for his go-ahead before the next. Do not run batches side by side.
9b. **Relationships are the centre.** A new decision or event says what it does to the people involved through `relBump()` and `youRemember()`, so they remember it and the notification bar reports it.
10. **Commits:** small, with a message that says what changed for the player. Tick the boxes in `TASKS.md` in the same commit.
10a. **Versions:** from 6 October, at least five changes go into a version before its number moves (`VER` in `app/src/store.ts`). Commit each change as it is done; bump the number once five or more are in.

## Things that bite

- `build.js` must run before any test; the tests open `dist/index.html` and `engine.js`.
- `calibrate()` in `src/00-core.js` runs sample shows for every promotion at the start of a game to set crowd expectations. A change to match scoring moves expectations with it, so compare "rating minus expectation", not raw ratings.
- The typeface (VT323) has no box-drawing, block or shape characters. They come from the generated font `EWF Blocks`. A new symbol in the interface needs adding to `tools/build-font.py`.
- A game year is 48 weeks: twelve months of four weeks (`cal()` in `src/00-core.js`). Yearly things use 48, not 52.
- Preact needs keys on lists whose items change shape between redraws.
- Browser tests start with this week's tasks as reminders only (`helper.open()` calls `E.setGate(S, false)`). Pass `gate: true` to test the stopping.
- A show's city is not picked on the night any more: it is the next stop on the road. `venueFor()` still draws and throws away one number so old seeds play out the same.
- The desk's and Backstage's type is a little smaller than other one-screen pages for the same window (the `:has(.deskpage)` and `:has(.bspage)` rules in `app/styles/win.css`).
- On a page of windows the flash note sits beside the page tabs and lets presses through (`.main:has(.desktop) > .flash` in `booking.css`).
- A pop-up puts the highlight on its closing button. Mark a control `data-first` to send it there instead (the clock a player has just come back from).
- The headless sim never signs anyone, so its rosters shrink over time. That is the sim, not the game.
- A call on the broadcast takes no answer for its first 400 ms, so a press meant for Continue cannot answer it. Browser tests wait before they pick.
- While a show is on the air the report is `S.live.st.rep`, not `S.reports[0]`. It is filed when the show goes off the air.
- On a one-screen page the frame does not scroll, so the menu and the bottom line must not be offset (`#app:has(.onescreen) .ftop{top:0}` in `booking.css`). A TV holds the bars in from the edge of the glass; with the offset left on, the menu lay over the top of the page. `fits(page)` now checks that neither bar lies over the page.
- A call waits out its own time before it can come up again (`S.lcd`, weeks by kind or by person). A test that wants a call sets up its cause and clears `S.lcd`; `app/tests/calls.js` shows how.
