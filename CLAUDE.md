# WrestleCraft: Be The Booker: working notes for Claude Code

WrestleCraft: Be The Booker is a wrestling booking sim for Steam that looks made by hand (rule 2a). Ryan renamed it on 19 October 2026; it was EWF Wrestling Manager from 7 October and EWF 9000 before that. The stored keys (`ewf9000-save-4`, `gorilla-position-prefs`) and the desktop build's save folder keep the old names on purpose (rule 9), so nobody's games go missing. The player's own promotion still defaults to the Elite Wrestling Federation (EWF), a company inside the game. The owner is Ryan. `NEXT.md` is the work queue: ordered steps for the next unattended run. `TASKS.md` is the task list and holds his decisions; `TWEAKS.md` is 100 small jobs and `WISHLIST.md` is 100 depth ideas, each with its own "how to work this list"; `docs/feature-list.md` is the full feature wording; `docs/design.md` is the design document; `app/DEV.md` says how screens are written.

## How the code is laid out

- `src/*.js`: the simulation. Plain ES5 in one shared closure, concatenated in file-name order by `build.js`. No screen code. State `S` is plain JSON and is the save file. A seeded generator (`rnd(S)`) makes every game repeatable. Systems plug in through hook lists declared at the top of `src/10-match.js` (`MQX`, `CRX`, `FINX`, `EFX`, `POST`, `SHOWX`, `WEEKX`, `NEWX`, `PREX`, `TASKX`).
- `src/66-relations.js`: the relationship matrix (`S.rm`) and what people remember (`S.rmY` for what they remember about the booker). It is the one place that says how two people feel about each other. Change it only through `relBump()` and `youRemember()`; read it with `relOf()`, `bondOf()`, `respOf()`, `jealOf()`. It never calls `rnd(S)`. `note(S, heading, line, kind)` puts a line on the notification bar. `docs/plans/gorilla-position.md` is the plan this belongs to.
- `src/30-show.js`, `src/31-live.js` and `app/src/screens/booking/Live.tsx`: the live show. The player's show is not worked out in one go. `E.liveBegin(S, card)` puts it on the air (`S.live`, plain data, so a save taken mid-show loads back into it), `E.liveNext(S)` runs the next promo, angle or match, and the show stops whenever the gorilla position needs a call (`{event}`) until `E.liveDecide(S, choice)` answers it. A kind of call is one entry in `LIVEK` (`pre` or `post` offers it from what is true of the next step, never a flat chance; `run` applies the answer through `relBump()`, `youRemember()`, feuds and titles). `livePlay()` runs a whole show with the safe answers, and `E.runPlayerShow` is that. Rival shows run straight through with no calls. The broadcast screen is one screen, laid out as television (Ryan, 19 October): the bar with ON AIR and the clock, tonight's order as a rail of chips under it (`Night`, `data-t="night"`: stars on what has been on, the one on now, where a call waits), the scene in a set with a chyron and the company's bug (`.tvframe`), the commentary as closed captions on the picture (`Captions`: the newest line and the one before it; Ryan, 21 October) and the call as captions under it, the result popping up over the scene's last moment when a segment ends (`.lv-res`, with the new champion's belt above it), and at the right the feed (`LiveFeed`: fans, the wrestlers, the press and the company posting as the show goes, `E.liveFeed()` in `src/94-livefeed.js`, hashed, never `rnd(S)`) with the crowd meter under it (`crowdWord()`). A show on the air cannot be skipped or left. The SPACE key at the right end of the bottom line is the only way forward: it says what it will do (Ring bell, Continue, Next, Sign off, Report, Your call) and holds the focus (`liveStep()` and `liveGo()` in `screens/booking/run.ts`). The broadcast has no continue button of its own. A promo, angle or match can be skipped to its result; a call from the gorilla position can never be skipped. Auto-play (`pref.auto`, on by default, P or the `live-auto` button; `autoArm()` in `run.ts`; pace `pref.pace` is TV, full, quick or results; a promo or an angle plays through on one press even with auto-play off; TV, the default, gives a match about four seconds per minute it runs on the clock and other segments one second per minute it runs on the clock and shows only its big moments, its finish and evenly spaced lines (`tvKeys()`, `tvNext()`), so a two-hour show runs about two minutes; the `live-pace` button and Options) moves the broadcast on at a reading pace, lingers on a big moment (a beat with `h`: 1 for a near fall or a finish, 2 for a new champion, set in `callMatch()`), and never rings the opening bell, answers a call or leaves the sign-off. Browser tests run with it off unless `open({ auto: true })`; `app/tests/auto.js` tests it.
- `src/34-night.js` and `app/src/screens/booking/After.tsx`: After the show, where a show ends. The sign-off does not lead to the match-by-match report. It leads to the After the show screen (`book().night`; `liveDone()`, `openNight()`, `closeNight()`, `nightFull()` in `run.ts`): the grade and the verdict, the matter the night left that needs an answer (it can be answered there), and a list of what else it left with the picked one shown beside it. The report is one press away (Match by match) and closes back to this screen. The SPACE key says Office: on to the desk, where Before the show is the first thing. The Office has no After the show panel; it has one button, After the show recap, that comes back. When a show goes on the air the engine notes how everybody stood (`S.live.n0`), and when it goes off the difference is filed as `rep.left`: the gate, television, who got hurt, whose mood moved, what the notification bar said, what the writers thought. `E.afterShow(S)` lays it out, trouble first. A new thing a show should leave behind is one more `add()` in `E.afterShow`. A show can also leave one matter in the inbox that has to be answered (`nightMatter()`: a hurt champion, somebody furious, a lost belt and the rematch, an act that caught fire, an upset nobody called, a main event that was the weakest match, the match of the night with no story); a new one is one more case there and an `EVR` entry.
- `src/32-direction.js`: the calls that set the direction of the company. The owner on the headset (the office wants its favourite to win: do as told, refuse, or find a way round). Who the show is built around (`S.fc = {id, w}`, decided on the air after a main event; read it with `faceOf(S)` or `E.faceInfo(S)`). The crowd comes to see the face of the company: a lift to the show's hype when they are in the main event, a letdown when they could be on the card and are not (`faceHype()`, and a warning from `E.validate`), and louder matches. The network on the line (what is going out is more than the slot was sold as; the answers move the network's patience, `S.clocks.net`). The sponsor at ringside (a deal with four weeks or fewer left wants its name on the main event). A new call of this kind is one more entry in `LIVEK` here, added to `LIVE_ORDER` (before a segment) or `LIVE_POST` (after one).
- `src/33-ring.js`: three calls caused by the match in the ring. Somebody is hurt (a worn body in a demanding match; the answers are the old headset's bad landing, through `E.resolveChaos`). The crowd has gone quiet (the match before fell flat: stay, take it to the floor, the villain cheats, or call the upset). They are not going home (two who work well together, a hot feud or creative control: take it home, or give them the time and cut a segment, shorten the main event or run over the slot). The order every kind is tried in is `LIVE_ORDER`, set at the end of this file.
- `src/99-overall.js` and `app/src/shared/ovr.tsx`: the overall rating (Ryan, 20 October), one number from 1 to 99 spread the way the sports games spread it (Ryan, 21 October: a handful reach 90, a main-eventer is in the 80s, the middle of a card in the 60s; `overallRaw()` is the old scale that wages, signings and levels still use) for sorting (ring work most, then the crowd, the microphone, stamina, how steady and how safe), worked out each time and never kept (`E.overallOf(w)`). `w.ovr` is still how over they are with the crowd. `Ovr` draws it as a badge coloured by tier; the Roster and Free agents sort by it first.
- `src/99-progress.js` and `app/src/screens/roster/Attrs.tsx`: progression (Ryan, 20 October; `docs/plans/progression.md`). Every wrestler has a level (`w.lv`), experience (`w.pxp`), a development trait (`w.dev`: Normal, Rising star, Blue-chip, Generational) and points to spend (`w.ap`) on eleven attributes from 1 to 100. Experience comes from each show (`SHOWX`, stars squared, a five-star bonus), points from levels, and doing it grows it (`w.gx`: hardcore matches teach Hardcore, promos the Microphone). Caps by potential and age (`progCap`): the body stops growing past the peak, the microphone never does. Rivals spend at once (`progAuto`); the player spends in the Attributes pop-up (`E.spendAttr`, `E.autoSpend`; `E.spendPoint` is the booker's own skill point on Career), and points waiting eight weeks are spent for them. After the show lists who gained (`rep.prog`). The asking wage counts the overall rating. The match's old experience line in `src/10-match.js` is gone; training (camps, classes, trainers) still adds to `w.xp` as before.
- Regression (Ryan, 21 October), in `src/99-progress.js`: age (the yearly decline, and consistency and Injury past the cliff), ring rust (eight weeks without a match, a point every four weeks, three a spell), a cold streak (three poor matches in a row) and a long injury (six weeks out or more) take points away through `progLose()`, kept on the wrestler as `w.rgx` and shown in the Attributes pop-up and After the show. The attribute names on screen are Agility (`speed`) and Injury (`safe`: how safely they work).
- `src/99-contracts.js`: contracts by the size of the company (Ryan, 21 October). A big company (image 65 or more) signs yearly deals (`w.dl` 'year', one to three years, a salary every week); a small one (under 50) pays per appearance (`w.dl` 'show', `w.fee` for each show they are on, a handshake that rolls on at 12 weeks so anybody can make an offer); a mid-sized one puts the top third of its card on yearly deals. `payWeek()` is the week's real wage bill in `settle()`; the wage budget still reads `w.wage`. `E.contractOf`, `E.offerTerms`, `E.signFee`. Pay follows the size of the company (`PAY_TOP`, `payTop()`, `payRatio()` and `wageFor()` in `src/00-core.js`): the top of the card earns about $3M a year at the biggest company down to about $25k at a small indie, and the parody world's salaries are set on the same scale. `calibrateCosts()` gives a small company a margin of up to 8% of its income, since a small company's ordinary week falls short of its average more often. Income follows the size too (`msc(P)` in `src/00-core.js`, set by the size a company started at): the biggest company takes about $35M a week and a small indie about $25k; pay-per-view and merchandise scale in full, the television audience and money by its square root each, crowds by a quarter power and ticket prices by 0.3, so an indie still draws a few hundred at about $20. Starting cash, production and target profit scale as income does (about `msc` to the 0.75). A game saved before this (no `P.ms`) keeps the old money.
- `src/99-gimmicks.js`: smart gimmicks. Each of the fourteen gimmicks (`GIMS` in `src/60-rpg.js`) carries in `GFX` the rules it shines in, the kinds of match, hot and flat pairings, its promo strength and how it plays in a main event; it moves the crowd (`CRX`), feud heat (it wraps `heatUp`) and promos (it wraps `segMean`). A new game gives everybody the gimmick their character's words point to (`GIM_WORDS`), else the best fit. `E.gimInfo(S, id)` is what the Attributes pop-up shows.
- `src/99-staff.js` and `app/src/screens/roster/Staff.tsx`: staff (Ryan, 20 October). People on your books who do not wrestle (`w.nw`) with a job (`w.srole`, `SROLES` in `src/85-second.js`: road agent, trainer, commentator, manager, on-screen boss, and now referee and interviewer). Create staff (`E.createStaff`, the Staff button on the Roster and Free agents) hires one straight onto your books; Move to staff (`E.toStaff`, from a wrestler's file) takes a wrestler off the card, and Wrestle again (`E.fromStaff`) puts them back. Each job carries the tag scenes look for (`STAFF_TAG`), and a commentator takes a seat in the desk's pool (`S.voices`, `wid`). A new job is one more entry in `SROLES` and `STAFF_TAG`.
- `src/99-titleshow.js`: a title for one weekly show (Ryan, 20 October). `t.show` is a show's id: on weekly television the title is defended only there (it wraps `showTitles`), and big events can still put it on. Set on the Titles page (Defended on, when there is more than one show) with `E.setTitleShow`. The belt itself is Create a Belt's.
- `src/79-people.js` and `app/src/screens/office/Backstage.tsx`: Backstage is its own page in the Office (the desk has one line about it and a button), built as the board (`docs/mockups/final/final-2-backstage.png`, Ryan's pick): one screen of five windows. Who needs you tonight (everybody in the building for a reason, a line each with a face, a mark, mood and where they stand with you; worst first, by room or your door first; as many lines as the window holds, then pages), the file of whoever is picked down the right (`DossierBody`, which says less, one step at a time, when its window is short), Things to do (a few buttons a page, each opening its pop-up), At your door (requests answered there, free) and The room. It is people, not rooms. Seven rooms: Your office first (the old production truck, id `truck`: people come to the booker's door about their story), catering, the trainer's room, the gym, the parking lot, the owner's office, wrestlers' court. Nobody is in a room by chance: `peopleNow(S)` puts each person in the room their reason belongs to (hurt or worn down in the trainer's room; furious, low, real heat or the owner's favourite in catering; a rival's offer, a contract running down or an ambush in the parking lot; the breakout pick, an act the crowd is with or two rivals who do not click in the gym; a rivalry's next chapter, the champion, a newcomer or the face of the company in the truck). It only reads the game. One action point is spent on a person, once a week each (`E.peopleDo`, `S.apWho`), and what it does is an entry in `PPL_ACT`, through `relBump()` and `youRemember()`. A matter the last show left on the desk can be answered there (sitting down with somebody furious closes it). A new reason is one more block in `peopleNow()`. People also come to Your office to ask for something (kinds starting `ask`: a title shot, out of a rivalry, a turn, a team): the answers are free (`free: 1`), yes does the real thing, no and never being seen are remembered, and nobody asks again for twelve weeks (`S.lcd['ak' + id]`). Seven kinds so far: a title shot, out of a rivalry, a turn, a team, more money, two weeks at home, and the ending of a ripe story on the next big night (`askend`, which takes the place of the next-chapter visit). A scene is two people in a room together (`duo`; the screen names both): a team arguing at your door (`argue`), somebody jealous of another's spot (`jealous`), a veteran working late with a young one (`late`). A new scene is one more block in `peopleNow()` that marks both people as seen. The rooms' own actions (the owner, the court, the class, the pep talk, the truck) are still under "Around the building", one visit to each a week. Each person has a file (`src/79-dossier.js`, `E.dossier(S, id)`; the pop-up is `DossierWindow` in `screens/office/Dossier.tsx`, opened by Their file): why they are in the building, mood and how it moved since the last show (`S.moodAt`, written when your show goes off the air), where they stand with you, stress, the last three things they remember about you, two closest friends and two worst rivals, their stories, and the answers open to you with chance and cost. `E.roomInfo(S)` is the room in one line at the top of Backstage (trust, mood, unhappy, hurt, circles, the worst heat).
- `src/76-friends.js`: a friend of the champion becomes the next in line for their title (`friends`, once a pair, twelve weeks apart): a title match done with respect, let it turn sour (a feud), or keep them apart.
- `src/76-student.js`: when somebody a wrestler trained (`w.coach`) becomes more over than them, it reaches the desk once (`passed`): the teacher puts the student over, leave it (the teacher grows jealous), or teacher against student.
- `src/21-living.js`: living storylines (`docs/plans/living-storylines.md`, Ryan 14 October; it replaced the short, medium and long feuds). A feud has no length and no planned week: it has heat (how much the crowd cares) and freshness (`f.fresh`, how much it still has to give). Every beat raises heat and costs freshness, the same kind of beat again is worth less (`feudHeatRoom()`), and a twist or a turn or a week of rest gives freshness back. One-sided feuds and people in two feuds heat slower; real heat in the relationship matrix heats faster. It ends when the booker ends it: a match marked End the story here (`m.end`, `E.setMatchEnd`) or an ending planned for a night (`f.endAt`, `E.storyPlanEnd`); `feudDue()` says 'end' then, and 'chapter' for a round on a big night (`closeChapter()`). Suggest a card marks the ending of a ripe feud (hot and nearly used up) on a big night. The ending pays by the moment (`feudPayMult()`: heat, freshness and how long it lived). A feud that never gets going, stays cold for four weeks, or is left alone ten weeks fizzles (`feudFizzle()`). `E.feudPlan(S, f)` is everything the screens show (phase in words: Spark, Building, Boiling, Going stale, Cooling); `E.season(S)` is the heat line of every feud over the last twelve weeks and what the state says. Start a feud costs 2 booking power and asks only for two people (`E.storyPreview`, `E.storyStart`). Rival companies do not keep feuds yet; `rivalRipe()` is ready for when they do.
- `src/98-plot.js` and `Plot` in `app/src/screens/stories/Storylines.tsx`: booking power spent on the Storylines page. Three entries in `PLOT`: break up a tag team (2), push a wrestler (2, once in eight weeks each), buy a budget pre-tape (1). A pre-tape is an ordinary segment on the next show's run sheet marked `tape: 1`, placed at the start, the middle or the end, and a suggested card keeps it. `E.plotInfo(S)`, `E.plotDo(S, id, o)`. A new thing booking power buys there is one more entry.
- The rooms' own actions backstage (`E.apDo`) are buttons under "Things to do" on the Backstage page. Each opens a pop-up (`ActWindow`, modal kind `apact`) that says what it does, asks who, and spends the point. There is no menu of rooms.
- `src/78-models.js`: the nine company models. Each is data plus small functions for match quality, crowd, finishes, pushes, hiring fit and the suggested card.
- `app/src/`: the interface in Preact and TypeScript. `store.ts` holds the game and view state, `nav.ts` the screen map, `input.ts` screen modes and remote or gamepad focus, `kit/` the building blocks, `screens/<section>/` one folder per section, `shared/` pieces used by more than one section.
- `src/86-editor.js` and `app/src/screens/editor/`: the World Editor, opened from the title screen. Create a Wrestler (Create a Player, `app/src/screens/player/`) and Create a Scene (`app/src/screens/scene/`) are its foundation (Ryan, 18 October); the rest of the editor is built around them (TASKS.md 0y). It edits a universe package (plain JSON kept with the player's other worlds), never a running game. Every change goes through an `E.ed*` function so it can be tested headless.
- `src/87-create.js` and `app/src/screens/manage/Create.tsx`: belts and weekly shows made during a game. These are big moments, not yearly habits, and they follow real-life precedent: each takes an occasion (`beltOccasions()`, `showOccasions()`), a company adds one belt a year and one show in two at most, and rivals add them rarely, one at a time, only for something that has come about since the game began (`rivalGrow()`, once a year in each rival's own month; `S.mkw` spaces them across the world). `P.mk` is where a company stood, `P.grown` its record. A new occasion is one more entry in those two functions. A world can carry backdrops made in Create a Scene's Backdrop tab (`pkg.backdrops`, `kit/backdrop.ts`): a scene whose set is `bg_` and a backdrop's id stands in front of it in both renderers; `useBackdropSource()` says where they come from (the world being edited, or `E.worldBackdrops(S)` in a game), and `setKey()` keeps the set caches fresh while one is edited.
- `src/86-looks.js`, `app/src/kit/caw.ts` and `app/src/screens/player/`: Create a Player (`docs/plans/create-a-player.md`, Ryan's blueprint of 18 October). A wrestler made there is one small record in Ryan's shape (`wrestler.json`: body, three outfits, lettering and shapes, finishing moves), kept on the worker in a world (`workers[i].look`, `E.edSetLook`), carried onto the wrestler in a game (`w.look`) and written out as a `.wrestler.json`. `E.lookClean()` keeps only what it knows. `recipeOf(w)` builds the paper doll from it (`fromLook()`); a scene dresses each person for its set (`wear()`, `ATTIRE_OF`: ring gear in the ring, entrance gear on the ramp, street clothes backstage). Every part is cut by `puppet()` to fit the body; colours are swatches with a finish (`SWATCHES`: matte, foil, corrugated, neon, denim, leather; `Piece.fin`). A new part is one entry in `PARTS` and a few lines in `puppet()`; a new shape one case in `shape()`. A winner with a finishing move ends the broadcast on that move's picture. The finisher plays in the match through a `FINX` hook at the end of `src/86-looks.js`: people kick out of finishers, rarer the more protected the move, and each kick-out wears it down (`w.fko`, back a step a month); a clean win with it lands by protection, impact and position. Never `rnd(S)`. The suite opens full screen from a wrestler's page in the World Editor (Edit wrestler), from a wrestler's file on the Roster page in a game (Edit wrestler, modal kind `player`, `E.setLook`), and from Create a wrestler on Free agents (Create a Player, `E.createWrestler`): a wrestler made in a game always goes to free agency and is signed like anybody else; only the World Editor puts somebody into a company (Ryan, 18 October). Each wrestler picks an entrance (`look.entrance`: one of the canned `ent()` scenes in `scene.ts`, or a custom one made from the suite's Make a custom entrance in the World Editor), with pyro, lights and the name of their music on top (`look.entrance_fx`, `dressEntrance()` in `kit/scene.ts`; the announcers name the music in the entrance line); every wrestler has one finisher and two signature moves (Ryan, 19 October; `threeMoves()` in `kit/caw.ts` fills any missing, the signatures by style), each with the scene it plays as, and each signature with when in a match it comes out (`stage`: early, the middle, late); up to three taunts (`look.taunts`, scenes of the kind `taunt`) play now and then on the broadcast instead of a move. Every doll has a front and a back (Ryan, 19 October): `Place.b` and `Fig.b` draw it from behind (`headBack()` and the back branch of the torso in `puppet()`); lettering placed `on: 'back'` shows only from behind, and lettering on the chest or leg only from the front.
- The World Editor's Scenes tab is three studios (Ryan, 20 October), one editor set up three ways (`STUDIOS`, `studioOf()`, `setupsFor()` at the end of `kit/scene.ts`; `SceneStudio` takes `mode`): **Animation** for anything that moves, **Create a Move** for a move in the ring (setups from a clothesline, a spear, a suplex, a powerbomb and a DDT to the classic moves off the top, `CLASSIC`; a new move is `when: 'move'`), and **Create a Scene** for one picture from a template (`TEMPLATES`, `templateScene()`: a poster, a magazine cover, a video game cover, an action shot, a portrait, the champion with the belt). Stock scenes move in four keyframes or more (`flourish()`: a wind-up, then an overshoot and a settle). The 3D stage (`kit/diorama3d.ts`) builds the ring in an arena, a ballroom, a gym or a stadium (`VENUE`) with a steel cage, the cell with its roof, an inferno round it or a belt hung over it for a ladder match (`RIG`), ringside on the floor, and rooms for the entrance stage, backstage, catering, the trainer's room, the weight room, the parking lot, a talk show, a news desk and the rafters (`ROOMS`); a prop standing on its own is a 3D model (`MODELS3`), one in somebody's hands stays card. A match booked under steel cage, steel cell (a cage with a roof), ring of fire or ladder rules (`STIP` in `src/00-core.js`) is played in that set on the broadcast (`RIG_OF` in `Live.tsx`). The stock moves are scenes of their own too (`MOVES`, ids `move_<kind>`), so a wrestler's finisher or signature can play any of them (`FIN_SCENES` in `kit/caw.ts`), and Make a custom move on Create a Wrestler's Moves tab opens Create a Move with them in it; saving makes it that move's picture. The prop Words (`words`) is lettering with nothing behind it, in any colour, with a thin outline so it reads on any picture (`txt.edge`). A new set is one entry in `SETS` and a builder here; a new prop model is one entry in `MODELS3`.
- `src/93-segworld.js` and `app/src/screens/editor/Stories.tsx`: the World Editor's Stories tab. A world carries its own promo and angle kinds (`pkg.segkinds`, ids `c_...`; carried into a game as `S.db.segkinds` and merged into `SEGK` by `segkSync()`): Character A and B, the scene it plays (`segPick()` uses it), what it does (`SEGFX`, `SEGHEAT`, `SEGBOND`, a lift) and the report line with `{a}` and `{b}`. An entry with the id of an included kind changes that kind for the world (name, description, character labels, scene, report line; what it does stays as included, `SEGK0`). `E.edSegKinds`, `E.edSetSegKind`, `E.edNewSegKind`, `E.edOverSegKind`, `E.edRemoveSegKind`.

- Every booking choice is a scene (rule 9c): a booked promo or angle plays `seg_<kind>` (`SEG_SCENES`), and every angle the story director writes carries its beat and its people (`beat`, `bids`, `beatTag()` in `src/20-story.js`; `E.BEATS` lists them) and plays `beat_<beat>` (`BEAT_SCENES`), or `seg_<beat>` where it is the same moment. `storyPick()`, `segPick()` and `beatPick()` in `shared/moment.tsx` cast them; the run sheet (`SegPic`) and the broadcast use the same pick. ## Rules

0. **This is a Steam game, not a browser game** (Ryan, 18 October: "that is the core rule and standard"). The desktop build on Steam (`desktop/`, Electron) is the product, judged on a desktop screen. **1080p is the base and every layout scales up to the main modern resolutions** (1440p, 4K, ultrawide) (Bill, 22 October: "THE GAME WILL 100% SCALE up"). There is no Steam Deck or tablet layout (Bill, 22 October). The browser page and the live artifact are for testing and showing work, not the bar to build to. **The diorama renderer is the graphics engine of the game** (`app/src/kit/paper.ts`, `puppet.ts`, `scene.ts`; Create a Scene in the World Editor): how good its pictures look is how good the game looks. Build it to that standard: real depth (card thickness, light across each piece, shadows that grow with height, soft floor shadows, depth of field), smooth at 60 frames a second on a modest PC, with a quality setting for slower machines. `docs/plans/papercraft-engine.md` is the plan.
   **Platforms (Ryan, 18 October): Steam first, Android down the road.** Build and test for the Steam desktop: 1080p first, then 1440p and 4K (`MODES=desk,big` runs the 2560 by 1440 pass; `onescreen.js` also checks 3840 by 2160). Do not test or lay out for tablet or TV modes: do not add tablet or TV checks or rules. Keep every control working by touch as well as by mouse, keys and pad (rule 5), because Android comes later.
1. **The engine owns the game.** A screen never writes to `S`. It calls `E.something(S, ...)` inside `act()` and redraws.
2. **External assets are allowed** (Ryan, 23 October: "we can have external assets", a new rule; it replaces "zero external assets"). Recorded sound effects, music, images and other files made outside the code can ship with the game, as files beside the page like the soundtrack's recordings (`app/music/` copied to `dist/music/`). Every asset must be free to use in a commercial Steam game (public domain, CC0, or a licence that allows it), and its source and licence go in a credits file beside the tool that brings it in (`tools/music/CREDITS.md` is the pattern). Ship them with the game: never load anything from another site while the game runs, so it still plays offline. Fonts stay embedded.
2a. **The look is the workbench, made by hand** (Ryan, 19 October: "we are switching to concept a"; it replaces the scrapbook zine chosen earlier that day and the construction paper Macintosh of 18 October). Every page has to look as if somebody made it by hand with art supplies, the same hand-crafted feel as the diorama:
   - **The board** is black construction paper (`--lk-black`). Everything on a page is cut paper laid on it, with no tape: windows are torn cream paper; title strips are navy card lettered in yellow marker; buttons are cut from coloured card (green, yellow, blue, navy, red), each set a touch off straight; option tiles are index cards and sticky notes.
   - **No dot matrix** (Ryan, 19 October): maps are states cut from card, lettering that was in lights is marker on black card, pictures are painted card.
   - **The broadcast** is a black card mat in a thick white card frame; the call is handwriting on a card under the picture; the feed is index cards, with a sticky note when a wrestler posts.
   - **Lettering** is by hand: Permanent Marker for titles, buttons and labels; Patrick Hand for running text, chosen because it is the easiest of the hand faces to read; Gochi Hand only for small notes and doodles. Figures in tables may stay in the clean face. The faces are embedded (`app/fonts/hand/`, Apache 2.0 and OFL licences in `LICENSES.md`).
   - **The pieces** are made by `tools/creator/craft-kit.py` (textures and cut pieces drawn from noise and geometry, repeatable) into `app/ui/craft/`, embedded by `build.js` as `--lk-*`, and drawn as `border-image` frames; `app/styles/craft.css` comes after everything else and dresses every page. Blender (`bpy`) and hand-made PNG assets are allowed wherever they make a piece look more real. A new piece is one more call in the kit and one rule in `craft.css`.
   - **Wrestlers are clean**: flat colour, no shading on them; they throw one shadow on the set. **No CRT filter, no scan lines.**
   - **Modern design makes choices easy to pick** (Ryan, 19 October): anything you can choose is clearly a thing to press. It lifts and straightens when pointed at, shows a clear focus ring for keys and pads, and says what it does. Smooth motion, hover lift, tips, highlights and layered depth are all welcome when they keep the hand-made look.
   - **Pop-ups for most options** (rule 6a): a page shows short buttons and tiles; the choices themselves open in a pop-up.
   - **Everything is very readable**: dark ink on light paper, never text straight on the cork without a paper or a shadow under it, running text in the readable hand at a comfortable size, one or two words on a button with the rest in its tip.

   The concepts that lost (B the cardboard box, C the scrapbook zine) are kept in `app/styles/looks/` and `app/ui/b`, `app/ui/c`; `EWF_LOOK=b|c EWF_OUT=look-b node build.js` builds one beside the real game. Under the craft look sit the earlier layers: `app/styles/theme.css` (the paper Macintosh of 18 October: Blender card pieces in `app/ui/*.png`, the Inter face, `--ch`, the tip slip in `shell/Tip.tsx`) and the window kit of 6 October (`app/src/kit/win.tsx`). Build new pages to the craft look, and move old ones one at a time, with Ryan's go-ahead for each.

3. **Original content only.** Models and storylines may follow how real companies and angles worked. Game content never names a real promotion, wrestler, stable, title or event. The default universe is public domain: people who died before 1926, myth and folklore, or fiction published before 1929 by an author who died before 1955. No figures still honoured in a living religion. No real-world rosters in this repository. Exception (Ryan, 16 October, on his lawyer's advice): a parody universe sits beside the public-domain one. It parodies real wrestlers and companies under changed, joking names, never the real name or a near-copy, with no real logos or photos, and the game shows a parody disclaimer.
4. **No dice wording.** Checks are "attempts" with a percentage chance and a list of what helps and what hurts.
5. **Four inputs.** Every control is a real button, select or input, reachable by mouse, touch, keyboard and remote or gamepad. Nothing depends on hover. Give each control a `data-t` name; the tests find controls that way.
5. (continued) **No phone, no portrait.** The game is for a wide screen: the Steam desktop (rule 0). There is no phone layout and no phone test run. A tall narrow window shows one line asking for a wide one. Do not add narrow-screen layout rules.
5a. **Where things sit.** Top: the menu bar, with the company and the date at its right end and nothing laid over it, and the notification bar under it. Bottom: one line. The numbers and how they moved on the left, then three small keys (Help, Options, Music), then the SPACE key at the right end, which says what happens next. There is no other big button. Tasks live on the desk, nowhere else. Nothing else goes in those bars. The bottom line never wraps: check it at 1280 wide and on a Steam Deck (1280 by 800) when anything is added to it.
5b. **One screen, no scrolling.** Ryan wants every page to fit the window with nothing to scroll. A window may scroll inside itself where it holds a stream of reading (Web, Ryan 14 October: "we can use more scrolling tools"); the page itself never scrolls, and `fits()` lets a window marked `overflow-y:auto` through. A page that has been converted wraps itself in `.onescreen` (styles at the end of `app/styles/booking.css`): the frame becomes exactly one window tall and the page's parts share the height. On a one-screen page type is sized from the window's height as well as its width (`--fs` in `base.css`, the `:has(.onescreen)` rules), so about 29 lines always fit; pages that still scroll keep the old sizes until they are converted. The pattern is a compact list on the left, one line an item, and a pane on the right that shows whatever is selected, or a tab of notes when nothing is. A list that can outgrow its box turns pages (`Paged` in `screens/booking/Side.tsx`). Exception (Ryan, 20 October: "I don't want rosters and stuff like that on pages, I want it scrollable"): the lists of wrestlers (Roster, Free agents, the Locker room's windows) show everybody in a window that scrolls inside itself (`.lb.scroll`, headings kept in view). Test with `fits(page)` from `app/tests/helper.js` and `app/tests/onescreen.js`, at 1280x720 as well as larger. Converted so far: the card builder, the broadcast, After the show, the desk, Backstage, Storylines, Career, Roster, Locker room, Titles, Free agents, Web, History (each as a page of windows; History is the books in a list and the picked one beside it), Overview (the gauges, then its parts in a list and the picked one beside it), Finances (the money, the weekly net, and the books by tab), and World (as `.onescreen.winpage`: the old panels share the height and scroll inside themselves). The Manage pages are short tile pages that already fit. Do not add a page, a panel or a block of text to a converted page without checking it still fits. Pages not yet converted still scroll; convert them one at a time, with Ryan's go-ahead for each.
5c. **The wrestler is always on show** (Ryan, 18 October; the roster screens of SmackDown: Here Comes the Pain are the model). On a page about wrestlers (the World Editor's Wrestlers page, the Roster page, Free agents) the picked wrestler, or the first on the list when nobody is picked, is drawn full length by the diorama engine in the big window beside the list at all times, never an empty space. The World Editor's Wrestlers page is the pattern (Ryan liked it as built): the list on the left, the wrestler in the middle with a lights strip, Edit wrestler and Edit attire, and on the right their Biography, Company, Attributes and More.
5d. **World Editor pages: more dropdown menus than buttons** (Ryan, 20 October). On the World Editor and its studios (Create a Wrestler, Create a Scene, Create a Belt, the logo creator) a choice of one thing from a list in words is a dropdown (`Sel`), and so is which part of a page is showing. Buttons are for actions (Save, Add, Play) and for choices that are pictures (shapes, colours, plates, swatches) or that can be on together (tattoos, roles). Every World Editor page wears the same frame (`EdShell` in `screens/editor/shell.tsx`; the studios wear the same bar and note strip). A list that will keep growing (the scenes, the promos and angles) is one scrolling list with a search box instead (`ScrollMenu` in `screens/editor/parts.tsx`): what was made or changed for this world first and marked, then the Included ones (Ryan, 20 October: what ships with the game is called Included, never "the game's own"). An included scene, move, promo or angle can be opened, changed and saved for a world, and put back. Browser tests choose with `pick(page, t, v)` and read the choice with `picked(page, t, v)` from the helper, so a control can move between a button row and a dropdown without breaking them.
5e. **Clear, structured labels** (Ryan, 20 October). A field's label is the short name of what it holds, a noun of one to three words, the same word for the same thing on every screen: Name, Type, Participants, Character A, Character B, Level, Division, Holders, Company, Scene, Preview, Description, Report line, Effects. No questions ("Who fights for it"), no sentences ("How big a prize"), no "The first one". Explanations go in the placeholder, the option text or a note under the field, not in the label. Panels are titled the same way (Details, Effects, Preview, Champions). Use letters for people in a scene or segment (Character A, B, C), as the studios do.
6. **Six sections:** Office (The desk, Backstage, Storylines, Career), Booking, Roster (Roster, Locker room, Titles, Free agents), Web (one page, Ryan 14 October: the dirt sheet as a 90s newsletter, the feed as a 90s chat room, and Wrestle Search, six make-believe wrestling sites in `app/src/screens/net/Search.tsx` with the fan board among them; a new site is one entry in `SITES`), Manage (Operations, House, Deals), Company (Overview, Finances, World, History). The desk, Backstage and Storylines are the main tabs of the Office (Ryan, 6 October); there is no Stories section any more. Web is information only, like Company. The feed scrolls in its window, and the dirt sheet is drawn as a 90s newsletter on paper (Ryan, 14 October). Manage holds every choice; Company is information only; the desk opens on the road, with what needs an answer under it.
6a. **Choices are buttons that open pop-ups.** Ryan, 6 October: a page of choices is a page of buttons. Each button says what it is and what you get (or where it stands now), and pressing it opens a pop-up with the choices. A result closes back to the pop-up it came from (`BACK_TO` in `store.ts`: Manage, Storylines, Backstage). Done so far: Backstage ("Things to do", `ACT_UI` in `screens/office/BeforeShow.tsx` names each button by what it does), Storylines (`Plot`, `PlotWindow`), and all three Manage pages (`tiles()` and `ManageWindow` in `screens/manage/Operations.tsx`: a tile is an id, a name, what it is set to now, and the body of its pop-up). The buttons share the `.bsact` style. A browser test that wants the choices themselves laid out on the page sets `window.EWF_FLAT = 1` (see `app/tests/company.js`); `ensure(page, sel)` in the helper opens whichever button holds a control.
7. **Readable first.** Ryan's design goal is the pull of "one more turn" with screens that are easy to read and quick to act on. Every action shows its result at once. Prefer a pop-up or a short note to another panel.
7a. **No clean stopping point.** Office, Booking and the Show each hand the next one something unresolved (`docs/design.md`, section 2a, in Ryan's words). The week opens on a fire with a deadline. Booking is a risk and reward puzzle where chemistry, morale and fatigue are limits the player can see. The Show pays out and ends on a new problem or chance for next week. A feature that ends a phase on a clean slate is not finished: say what it hands to the next phase.
8. **Plain writing in the game and in docs.** Short sentences, no jargon, no em dashes in new text.
8a. **Stars, not percentages.** Stars are for after the bell: nothing is forecast in stars before a show (see `src/99-fog.js`). How good a match, promo or angle was is shown as stars (`stars()` in the kit, `starG()` in the engine), never as a number. A target about a match is worded in stars and checked with `starMeets()`. Chances stay percentages (rule 4).
8c. **A show is a letter grade and a word.** The letter is the show against its own crowd (Ryan, 6 October): an A beat what this crowd expected, a B is what they came for, and it is never a percentage. `crowdScore(rating, exp)` in `src/98-words.js` puts the gap on the grade scale; a filed show carries it as `rep.cs` and is read with `repCS(rep)`, `E.repGrade(rep)` on a screen. Never grade `rep.rating` directly. A target about a show is worded as a grade on that scale and checked with `gradeMeets()`. How good a show was, whoever it was for, and what a crowd expects are said in words on the ladder (a bomb, a dud, flat, decent, solid, strong, hot, red hot, blow-away, all-time classic): `E.expectWords(S)`, `E.showWord(v)`. How a show did against its crowd is `E.showVerdict(rating, exp)`: a headline, one line, and a band (`verdictBand()`) that the letter, the dirt sheet and the Net all follow, so they cannot disagree. New text about a show uses these, not a made-up phrase.
8b. **Money:** short form (`cash()`, `$1.2M`) in tables and lists; full form (`full()`, `$1,200,000`) for a single headline figure.
9. **Saves:** the save is `S` under the key `ewf9000-save-4`. A change that breaks old saves needs a version bump and a migration.
9a. **One step at a time.** Do one step, show Ryan the result, and wait for his go-ahead before the next. Do not run batches side by side.
9d. **Blueprint, questions, then approval** (Ryan, 23 October: a core rule; it applies with 9a). Before planning, changing files, writing code or running build scripts for any new game mode, system or major feature, go through three gates in order:
   1. **The blueprint.** Bring together everything known about it (the project, these notes, what Ryan has said) into one complete blueprint document that stands on its own: the states it moves through and what moves it, the data it keeps, how the screens flow, and how the money and the balance loop. Write it in `docs/plans/<feature>.md`.
   2. **The questions.** Give Ryan a detailed list of questions, sorted by topic, that test the idea: the edge cases, how a player will think and feel about it, what keeps them coming back for one more turn, and the odd situations it has to survive.
   3. **Approval.** Write no code and make no source files for it until Ryan has read the blueprint, answered the questions and said plainly to go ahead.
   Small fixes, polish, and things Ryan has already decided in detail do not need the three gates; a new mode, a new system or a major feature always does. When unsure, ask.
9b. **Relationships are the centre.** A new decision or event says what it does to the people involved through `relBump()` and `youRemember()`, so they remember it and the notification bar reports it.
9c. **Every booking choice is a scene** (Ryan, 19 October: the core mechanic, started once Create a Wrestler and Create a Scene are built out). Storylines and building a card are hooked into scenes. Every storyline we build, and every angle or promo a player can pick for a card, has a scene to go with it, so building a show is building out the scenes of the show. Arc storylines and moments that run over several weeks have scenes for each of their beats. The World Editor gets an easy builder so players can make their own storylines, angles and promos, each with its scenes. The match builder and the show on the air are redone in the paper look on top of this later. A new storyline, angle or promo kind is not finished until it has its scene. TASKS.md 0u is the plan. Each booked kind's scene is in `SEG_SCENES` (`kit/scene.ts`, `when: 'seg_<kind>'`); the broadcast plays it from the report's `booked` and `ids` (`scenePick()` in `Live.tsx`), and `app/tests/scene.js` fails when a kind in `SEGK` has none.
10. **Commits:** small, with a message that says what changed for the player. Tick the boxes in `TASKS.md` in the same commit.
10a. **Versions:** from 6 October, at least five changes go into a version before its number moves (`VER` in `app/src/store.ts`). Commit each change as it is done; bump the number once five or more are in.

## Things that bite

- `build.js` must run before any test; the tests open `dist/index.html` and `engine.js`.
- `calibrate()` in `src/00-core.js` runs sample shows for every promotion at the start of a game to set crowd expectations. A change to match scoring moves expectations with it, so compare "rating minus expectation", not raw ratings.
- The typeface (VT323) has no box-drawing, block or shape characters. They come from the generated font `EWF Blocks`. A new symbol in the interface needs adding to `tools/build-font.py`.
- A game year is 48 weeks: twelve months of four weeks (`cal()` in `src/00-core.js`). Yearly things use 48, not 52.
- Preact needs keys on lists whose items change shape between redraws.
- Browser tests start with this week's tasks as reminders only (`helper.open()` calls `E.setGate(S, false)`). Pass `gate: true` to test the stopping.
- A show's city is not picked on the night any more: it is the next stop on the road. `venueFor()` still draws and throws away one number so old seeds play out the same.
- The desk's and Backstage's type is a little smaller than other one-screen pages for the same window (the `:has(.deskpage)` and `:has(.bspage)` rules in `app/styles/win.css`).
- On a page of windows the flash note sits beside the page tabs and lets presses through (`.main:has(.desktop) > .flash` in `booking.css`).
- A pop-up puts the highlight on its closing button. Mark a control `data-first` to send it there instead (the clock a player has just come back from).
- The headless sim never signs anyone, so its rosters shrink over time. That is the sim, not the game.
- A call on the broadcast takes no answer for its first 400 ms, so a press meant for Continue cannot answer it. Browser tests wait before they pick.
- While a show is on the air the report is `S.live.st.rep`, not `S.reports[0]`. It is filed when the show goes off the air.
- On a one-screen page the frame does not scroll, so the menu and the bottom line must not be offset (`#app:has(.onescreen) .ftop{top:0}` in `booking.css`). A TV holds the bars in from the edge of the glass; with the offset left on, the menu lay over the top of the page. `fits(page)` now checks that neither bar lies over the page.
- A call waits out its own time before it can come up again (`S.lcd`, weeks by kind or by person). A test that wants a call sets up its cause and clears `S.lcd`; `app/tests/calls.js` shows how.
